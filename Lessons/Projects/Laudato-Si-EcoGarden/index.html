Day 1: Selection, Rounding & Exact Decimal Operations
The Math Behind Our Garden: Stewardship through Precision 0-8 min

Designing a community garden is more than choosing pretty flowers. It's an act of stewardship. In his letter Laudato Si', Pope Francis calls us to care for "our common home" by using the resources we're given wisely and generously. As landscape planners for this project, your team will practice that same stewardship, turning a limited budget into a garden that serves the whole school community.

Budgeting is where the math begins. Every plant, bag of soil, and decoration has a price, and your $100.00 budget is a hard limit, not a suggestion. Planners round prices to get a fast estimate before committing to a purchase, then calculate the exact cost of every item. A garden that goes over budget isn't good stewardship, no matter how beautiful the design.

Good stewardship also means understanding what you're caring for. Matching each plant to its sun or shade needs takes the same careful planning as the budget itself. Arranging every item in your garden layout with care matters too, since a plant tucked in the wrong spot wastes its potential just like one budgeting mistake wastes money.

When you round a price, add up a column, or divide the total cost by the number of plants, you're not just doing math for its own sake. You're practicing the discipline that turns good intentions into a garden that actually gets built, stays within budget, and serves the community it was designed for.

Key Terms

Rounded Estimate - a quick approximation found by rounding a price to the nearest whole dollar before multiplying, used to double-check your math before the exact numbers are in.

Line Total - the exact cost of one item, found by multiplying its exact price by the quantity you're buying.

Budget - a fixed amount of money set aside for a purpose. Here, it's a hard limit of $100.00 for the entire garden.

Grand Total - the sum of every Line Total added together, showing exactly how much your team spent.

Stewardship - the responsible and faithful management of resources, including money, time, and the earth itself.
