Day 1: The Ethical Auditor
Precision Is a Form of Stewardship 0-8 min

In the tradition of stewardship, we are called to be responsible caretakers of the resources entrusted to us - whether that is a household budget, a parish's Community Fund, or a community's shared water supply. In mathematics, we use rational numbers to represent these resources. A positive value indicates a surplus - having more than is needed - while a negative value indicates a deficit.

Being a "steward" means more than just counting; it means ensuring that every decimal point and every fraction is in its right place so that resources are never wasted. When we audit a ledger, we are practicing the virtue of justice by making sure our records reflect the truth of our impact on our neighbors.

In the Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25:14-30), servants are judged by how faithfully they manage what was entrusted to them. A modern steward - a parish bookkeeper, a family, or a community engineer - carries that same responsibility today, one signed number at a time.

Remember: Signed Numbers
  • Expenses are represented as negative rational numbers (for example, −$124.80).
  • Grants, credits, and bonuses are represented as positive rational numbers (for example, +$82.50).
  • A surplus is a positive ledger balance; a deficit is a negative one.
