Number System Vocabulary
What to do on this tab Read and copy each term into your notebook. Then try the 3 quick checks at the bottom - you get 2 tries per question before the answer is shown.
Rational Number

Any number that can be written as a fraction \(\dfrac{a}{b}\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are integers and \(b \neq 0\). This includes whole numbers, integers, fractions, and every terminating or repeating decimal.

Irrational Number

A number that cannot be written as a fraction of integers. Its decimal form goes on forever without ever settling into a repeating pattern. \(\pi\) and \(\sqrt{2}\) are irrational.

Terminating Decimal

A decimal that ends - it has a finite number of digits after the decimal point. \(0.75\) and \(0.5\) are terminating decimals.

Repeating Decimal

A decimal in which one or more digits repeat forever. Written with a bar over the part that repeats: \(0.333\ldots = 0.\overline{3}\), and \(0.8333\ldots = 0.8\overline{3}\) (only the 3 repeats).

Numerator & Denominator

In a fraction \(\dfrac{a}{b}\), \(a\) is the numerator (how many parts you have) and \(b\) is the denominator (how many equal parts make up the whole).

Equivalent Fractions

Fractions that name the same value even though they look different, like \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) and \(\dfrac{2}{4}\).

Where Rational Numbers Live: every whole number and integer is rational (write it over 1), and so is every fraction, terminating decimal, and repeating decimal. Irrational numbers are the only numbers left out.

Operation Vocabulary: A Quick Review
What to do on this tab You learned these terms in earlier grades - this is a refresher, not new material. Copy them into your notebook, then try the matching check.
Reciprocal

Flip a fraction upside down - the numerator and denominator swap places. The reciprocal of \(\dfrac{3}{4}\) is \(\dfrac{4}{3}\). Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal.

Common Denominator (LCD)

A shared denominator that lets you add or subtract fractions with different denominators. The least common denominator (LCD) is the smallest one that works.

Simplest Form (Lowest Terms)

A fraction where the numerator and denominator have no common factor left to cancel, other than 1. \(\dfrac{2}{3}\) is in simplest form; \(\dfrac{4}{6}\) is not.

Decimal Point Alignment

Lining up the decimal points of two numbers, place value by place value, before you add or subtract them - the habit that keeps decimal arithmetic from going wrong.

Comparing & Ordering Language
What to do on this tab Word problems about rational numbers often hide an inequality inside ordinary phrasing. Read each phrase carefully - "at least" and "at most" are not the same thing.
at least = greater than or equal to (\(\geq\))  ·  at most / no more than = less than or equal to (\(\leq\))  ·  between = strictly greater than one value and less than the other.

"Between \(-2\) and \(3\)" means anywhere in that stretch of the number line - not including the endpoints.

1. A shipping label says a package weighs "at least 2.5 kg." The weight is:
A. exactly 2.5 kg   B. greater than or equal to 2.5 kg   C. less than 2.5 kg   D. less than or equal to 2.5 kg

2. A recipe says to use "no more than \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup of sugar." The amount used is:
A. exactly \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup   B. greater than \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup   C. less than or equal to \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup   D. greater than or equal to \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup

3. A pool's water temperature stayed "between 0.5°C and 2°C" all week. Which temperature could NOT have occurred that week?
A. \(1.2^\circ\text{C}\)   B. \(0.5^\circ\text{C}\)   C. \(1.9^\circ\text{C}\)   D. \(0.8^\circ\text{C}\)

Critical Thinking

Give a number that is at least \(0.5\) but less than \(1\). Then explain how you know it fits both conditions.
