Why We're Reviewing This First
Rational Numbers asks you to classify numbers and move fluently between fraction form and decimal form. Neither of those is possible without two things already in place: knowing the sign rules for positive and negative numbers (from the Integers topic), and being comfortable with basic fraction mechanics – simplifying a fraction, and converting between mixed numbers and improper fractions. Neither of those gets re-taught here from scratch, so this page makes sure both are ready to go.
Building on Integers

Vocabulary You Need First
These are mechanical, "how fractions are built" words from earlier grades. (Words like rational number, terminating decimal, and reciprocal are this topic's own new vocabulary, so they're taught in the Rational Numbers Vocabulary & Literacy page, not here.)
Proper Fraction

A fraction where the numerator is smaller than the denominator – it's worth less than one whole.

\(\frac{3}{4}\)
Improper Fraction

A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator – it's worth one whole or more.

\(\frac{9}{4}\)
Mixed Number

A whole number and a proper fraction written together.

\(2\frac{1}{4}\)
Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The largest number that divides evenly into two or more numbers – used to simplify a fraction in one step.

The GCF of 8 and 12 is 4
Integer Sign Rules: The Fast Recap
Before combining any two signed numbers, ask yourself one question: same signs, or different signs? That single question decides everything below, whether the numbers are whole numbers, fractions, or decimals.
The Rules
OperationThe Rule
AdditionSame signs: add the absolute values and keep the sign. (\(-3+(-5)=-8\))
Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. (\(-10+4=-6\))
SubtractionKeep-Change-Change: keep the first number, change subtraction to addition, change the sign of the second number, then follow the addition rule above. (\(5-(-2)\) becomes \(5+2=7\))
Multiplication & DivisionSame signs: the result is positive. Different signs: the result is negative.
Worked Examples

\(-7 + (-4)\): same signs, add and keep the sign → \(-11\)

\(9 - (-3)\): keep-change-change → \(9 + 3 = 12\)

\((-6) \times (-5)\): same signs → positive → \(30\)

\((-18) \div 3\): different signs → negative → \(-6\)

Fraction Mechanics: Simplifying & Converting
Rational Numbers will ask you to convert fractions and decimals back and forth constantly. Both of the skills below are things you'll do on nearly every problem, so they need to be automatic, not something you have to puzzle out each time.
Simplifying a Fraction

Find the GCF of the numerator and denominator, then divide both by it.

Example: simplify \(\frac{18}{24}\). The GCF of 18 and 24 is 6. \(\frac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \frac{3}{4}\).

Improper Fraction → Mixed Number

Divide the numerator by the denominator. The whole-number quotient is the whole-number part; the remainder becomes the new numerator, over the same denominator.

Example: \(\frac{17}{5}\). \(17 \div 5 = 3\) remainder \(2\), so \(\frac{17}{5} = 3\frac{2}{5}\).

Mixed Number → Improper Fraction

Multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and place that total over the same denominator.

Example: \(4\frac{2}{3}\). \(4 \times 3 = 12\), \(12 + 2 = 14\), so \(4\frac{2}{3} = \frac{14}{3}\).
