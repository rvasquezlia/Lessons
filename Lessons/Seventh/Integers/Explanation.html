Relating Integers to Their Opposites; Absolute Value
Every integer has an opposite the same distance from \(0\) but on the other side of the number line. The absolute value of a number is that distance - always \(0\) or positive, never negative. To compare two integers, the one farther to the right on the number line is always greater, even if it "looks smaller" without its sign.

COPY INTO MATH NOTEBOOK
  • To find an opposite: flip the sign. The opposite of a positive number is negative, and the opposite of a negative number is positive.
  • To find an absolute value: drop the sign - the answer is always \(0\) or positive, because it measures distance, not direction.
  • To compare two integers: picture them on a number line. Whichever is farther right is greater, regardless of which has the bigger absolute value.

Adding Integers
Same signs: add the absolute values, keep the common sign. Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.
1. Addition Rules - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Same Signs: Add the absolute values and keep the common sign. \(5+3=8\)    \(-5+(-3)=-8\)
  • Different Signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger. Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. \(-7+4=-3\) (subtract \(7-4=3\); keep negative because \(|-7|>|4|\)). \(10+(-2)=8\) (subtract \(10-2=8\); keep positive because \(|10|>|-2|\)).
Visualizing Addition: \(-3 + 5 = 2\) - starting at 0, move left 3 units, then right 5 units to land at 2.

Subtracting Integers
Subtracting is adding the opposite: \(a - b = a + (-b)\). Rewrite the subtraction as addition of the opposite, then use the addition rules above.
2. Subtraction Rule: "Keep-Change-Change" - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK

To subtract an integer, add its opposite. Follow these three steps to rewrite the problem as addition:

  1. Keep the first number exactly as it is.
  2. Change the subtraction sign to an addition sign.
  3. Change the sign of the second number to its opposite.

Example: \(4 - (-3)\): Keep the 4, Change \(-\) to \(+\), Change \(-3\) to \(3\) → \(4 + 3 = \mathbf{7}\).

Example: \(-2 - 5\): Keep \(-2\), Change \(-\) to \(+\), Change \(5\) to \(-5\) → \(-2 + (-5) = \mathbf{-7}\).

Reading a Subtraction on the Number Line: \(-4 - 9 = -13\) - the arrow starts at \(-4\) and moves 9 units further left (in the negative direction).

Multiplying and Dividing Integers
Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative. This rule holds for both multiplication and division - division "undoes" multiplication, so the sign rule is identical.
3. Multiplication & Division Sign Laws - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Same Signs = Positive: \((+)\times(+)=(+)\), \((-)\times(-)=(+)\). Examples: \(6\times2=12\), \(-4\times(-3)=12\), \(-20\div(-5)=4\).
  • Different Signs = Negative: \((+)\times(-)=(-)\), \((-)\times(+)=(-)\). Examples: \(8\times(-2)=-16\), \(-9\times3=-27\), \(15\div(-3)=-5\).
