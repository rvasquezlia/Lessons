Concept Review: Every Rule in One Place
What to do on this tab Skim the reference boxes - if any rule feels shaky, go back to Explanation for the full worked examples. Then answer the 4 Check Your Understanding problems.

Integers are whole numbers and their opposites (\(\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\)). This guide provides the essential rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers.

1. Addition Rules
  • Same Signs: Add the absolute values and keep the common sign. \(5+3=8\)    \(-5+(-3)=-8\)
  • Different Signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger. Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. \(-7+4=-3\). \(10+(-2)=8\).
2. Subtraction Rule: "Keep-Change-Change"

To subtract an integer, add its opposite: keep the first number, change subtraction to addition, change the sign of the second number.

Example: \(4 - (-3) \rightarrow 4 + 3 = \mathbf{7}\).    Example: \(-2 - 5 \rightarrow -2 + (-5) = \mathbf{-7}\).

3. Multiplication & Division Sign Laws
  • Same Signs = Positive: \(6\times2=12\), \(-4\times(-3)=12\), \(-20\div(-5)=4\).
  • Different Signs = Negative: \(8\times(-2)=-16\), \(-9\times3=-27\), \(15\div(-3)=-5\).

Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Forgetting to flip the sign when subtracting a negative number (\(a - (-b) = a + b\), not \(a - b\)).
  • Assuming a sum of two integers is always positive or always negative - it depends on the signs and the absolute values.
  • Multiplying/dividing three or more negative integers: track the sign one pair at a time, don't guess from the total count.

Ana says \(-10 - 4 = -6\), reasoning that \(10 - 4 = 6\) and the sign is negative. Is she correct?

Ana treated this as a same-signs problem, but it isn't one - there's no second negative to combine with. Using Keep-Change-Change: \(-10 - 4 \rightarrow -10 + (-4)\). Now the signs are the same, so add the absolute values and keep the negative sign: \(10 + 4 = 14\), giving \(-14\).
