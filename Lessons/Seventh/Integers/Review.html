Review: The Number Line & Number Fluency

Everything you need to remember before we start Integers

How this page works Nothing here is graded – this is a warm-up. Integers is really about one new idea (numbers can go below zero) layered on top of skills you already have: reading a number line, and computing confidently. Work through the tabs in order, and use the practice tab honestly to see what needs another look before we start. Your teacher can see which tabs you opened and whether you reached the end, so it's worth actually doing.
Why We're Reviewing This First
Integers takes the number line you already know and extends it in a direction you probably haven't worked with much yet: below zero. Almost every mistake students make with integers isn't really about negative numbers – it's about the number line itself (which way is "bigger"?) or about arithmetic facts (like \(8 + 5\) or \(12 - 7\)) not being automatic yet. This page makes sure both pieces are solid first.
Building on Decimal Operations

Last unit's Decimal Operations topic built your addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division fluency. Integers doesn't replace those skills – it reuses them, just with numbers that can now be positive or negative. Vocabulary You Need First
These are the words that describe the number line itself – the stage the whole Integers topic plays out on. (Words like integer, opposite, and absolute value are brand new this topic, so they're taught in the Integers Vocabulary & Literacy page, not here.)
Number Line

A straight line where every point represents a number, in order from least to greatest as you move left to right.

A ruler is a number line
Origin

The point labeled 0 on a number line – every other number's position is measured starting from here.

0 is the origin
Order / Compare

Deciding which of two numbers is greater or less, usually written with the symbols \(<\) (less than) and \(>\) (greater than).

\(3 < 8\), \(8 > 3\)
Ascending / Descending

Ascending means arranged from least to greatest. Descending means arranged from greatest to least.

2, 5, 9 is ascending; 9, 5, 2 is descending
The Number Line: What You Already Know
You've used number lines since elementary school for whole numbers. Every rule you already know still applies once we add negative numbers – numbers get bigger as you move right, and smaller as you move left. That single rule is the entire key to Integers.

Ordering Numbers

To order numbers, picture them on the line: whichever number sits further right is greater, no matter how the numbers look written out. Order 14, 3, 27, 9 from least to greatest: \(3, 9, 14, 27\) – read straight off the number line, left to right.

Number Fluency Recap: Whole Numbers & Decimals
Every rule for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers still asks you to do this exact arithmetic underneath. If \(9 + 6\) or \(3.4 \times 2\) takes real effort to compute, that effort will make the new integer rules feel much harder than they actually are.
Quick Recap: The Four Operations
OperationReminderExample
AdditionCombine two amounts into a total.\(24 + 18 = 42\)
SubtractionFind the difference, or what's left after taking away.\(50 - 27 = 23\)
MultiplicationRepeated addition of equal groups.\(7 \times 6 = 42\)
DivisionSplitting a total into equal groups.\(72 \div 8 = 9\)
Decimal Reminder

Line up decimal points to add or subtract. Multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal by counting total decimal places. To divide by a decimal, shift both the divisor and dividend right until the divisor is a whole number. Resources & Next Steps
You're Ready for Integers When You Can...
  • Plot whole numbers on a number line and read one back correctly.
  • Order a list of numbers from least to greatest (and greatest to least).
  • Add and subtract whole numbers and decimals without a calculator.
  • Multiply and divide whole numbers and decimals confidently.
  • Explain, in your own words, what a real-life "opposite" pair looks like (above/below zero, credit/debt, up/down).

Extra Practice, Any Time
