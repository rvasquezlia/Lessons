Standards & Objectives

CCSS.Math.Content.7.NS.A.1a, 7.NS.A.1b, 7.NS.A.1c, 7.NS.A.1d, 7.NS.A.2b, 7.NS.A.2c & MP.1, MP.3, MP.4, MP.5, MP.7, MP.8.

  • Relate integers to their opposites and find absolute value.
  • Add integers using the rules for same and different signs.
  • Subtract integers by rewriting as adding the opposite.
  • Multiply and divide integers using the same-sign/different-sign rule.
  • Apply integer operations to real-world situations (finance, elevation, temperature, science, sports).
Multidisciplinary Connections
  • Geography & Earth Science: sea level, elevation, temperature.
  • Financial Literacy: bank balances, deposits/withdrawals, stock market gains/losses.
  • Physical Science: electric charge (electrons/protons).
  • PE & Sports Statistics: yardage gains/losses, cumulative scores.
  • Critical Thinking & Self-Assessment: error analysis, metacognitive reflection.
Materials Needed
  • One device per student for all six pages.
  • No calculators this quarter.
Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Forgetting to flip the sign when subtracting a negative number (\(a - (-b) = a + b\), not \(a - b\)).
  • Assuming a sum of two integers is always positive or always negative - it depends on the signs and the absolute values.
  • Multiplying/dividing three or more negative integers: track the sign one pair at a time, don't guess from the total count.
  • Confusing "greater absolute value" with "greater integer" when comparing two negatives.

Metacognition & Discussion

Reflection prompts and small-group discussion questions to use alongside any of the six pages - none of this is graded content on a student page, it's for you to drop into a warm-up, an exit ticket, or a mid-lesson pause.

Reflection Prompts (use as an exit ticket or journal stem)
  • "Where did I use Keep-Change-Change today, and how did I know when it was time to flip the sign instead of just subtracting straight across?"
  • "When I found an absolute value, did I ever accidentally write down the number itself instead of its distance from zero? How would I catch that mistake if I made it again?"
  • "When I multiplied or divided three or more negative numbers, how did I keep track of the sign - one pair at a time, or did I try to guess from how many negatives there were?"
  • "If I compare \(-20\) and \(15\) on a number line, which one is farther from zero, and which one is actually the greater number? Why aren't those the same question?"
  • "What's one problem today where my first answer was wrong? What did I notice that made me go back and find the mistake?"
Leveled Small-Group Discussion Prompts
TierPrompt
Building UnderstandingTake turns explaining to your partner why \(-8-(-6)\) is not the same as \(-8-6\). Use a number line to show what "subtracting a negative" actually does to your position.
ApplyingAs a group, find two different pairs of integers whose product is \(-24\). Without listing every possible pair, explain the sign pattern (one positive, one negative) that guarantees a negative product every time.
ExtendingDesign your own multi-step integer word problem with at least three operations (like a submarine or a stock price changing several times). Trade with another group - can they solve it correctly, and can they point to the exact step where a sign error would be easiest to make?

Scaffolded Support Problems

For a student who is still guessing at sign rules rather than reasoning through them. Read each hint aloud before they attempt the problem - unlike the graded Practice Set items, the scaffolding is meant to be visible here, not discovered.

ProblemHint to giveAnswer
1. \(-9-(-4)\)Subtracting a negative is the same as adding its opposite - rewrite it as addition first, then solve.\(-9+4=\mathbf{-5}\)
2. \(|-15|\)Absolute value answers "how far from zero," not "what's the sign." Distance is never negative.\(\mathbf{15}\)
3. \(-7\times(-6)\)Same signs multiply to a positive answer - multiply \(7\times6\) first, then apply the sign rule.\(\mathbf{42}\)
4. \(-3\times(-2)\times(-5)\)Track the sign one pair at a time: multiply the first two and note the sign, then multiply that result by the third number.\(-3\times(-2)=6\); \(6\times(-5)=\mathbf{-30}\)

ProblemAnswer / Notes

Challenge Bank: One Level Harder

Extra problems for early finishers or homework extension - one step above Practice Set/Word Problems difficulty (triple operations, missing-integer puzzles, multi-step scenarios). Click a problem to reveal its solution.

Opposites, Absolute Value, Add & Subtract - Harder Tier

C1. Find the opposite of the opposite of \(-17\).

Show solution

Opposite of \(-17\) is \(17\); opposite of \(17\) is \(-17\). Taking the opposite twice returns the original number: \(\mathbf{-17}\).

C2. Find \(|-8| + |5|\).

Show solution

\(|-8| = 8\) and \(|5| = 5\). \(8 + 5 = \mathbf{13}\).

C3. Evaluate \(-34 + 19 - (-12)\).

Show solution

\(-34 + 19 = -15\). \(-15 - (-12) = -15 + 12 = \mathbf{-3}\).

C4. A submarine at \(-215\) m dives another \(180\) m, then rises \(95\) m. What is its final depth?

Show solution

\(-215 - 180 = -395\). \(-395 + 95 = \mathbf{-300}\) m.

C5. Which has the greater absolute value: \(-45\) or \(38\)?

Show solution

\(|-45| = 45\), \(|38| = 38\). Since \(45 > 38\), \(\mathbf{-45}\) has the greater absolute value.

Multiply & Divide Integers - Harder Tier

C1. Evaluate \(-6 \times (-4) \times (-3)\).

Show solution

\(-6 \times (-4) = 24\) (same signs, positive). \(24 \times (-3) = \mathbf{-72}\) (different signs, negative).

C2. Evaluate \(-144 \div (-12)\).

Show solution

Same signs, so the quotient is positive: \(144 \div 12 = \mathbf{12}\).

C3. A weather station records a temperature change of \(-4^\circ\text{C}\) every hour for 5 hours. What is the total change?

Show solution

\(-4 \times 5 = \mathbf{-20}^\circ\text{C}\).

C4. Evaluate \(-8 \times 7 \div (-2)\).

Show solution

\(-8 \times 7 = -56\). \(-56 \div (-2) = \mathbf{28}\) (same signs, positive).

C5. Find two integers whose product is \(-36\) and whose sum is \(-5\).

Show solution

Try factor pairs of \(36\) with opposite signs: \(4\) and \(-9\) give \(4 \times (-9) = -36\) and \(4 + (-9) = -5\). The integers are \(\mathbf{4}\) and \(\mathbf{-9}\).

Error Analysis - Harder Tier

C1. A student computed \(-18 - (-25)\) and got \(-43\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

The student subtracted \(25\) instead of adding it (didn't flip the sign). Correct: \(-18 - (-25) = -18 + 25 = \mathbf{7}\).

C2. A student computed \(-9 \times (-3) \times (-2)\) and got \(54\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

The student likely stopped tracking sign after the first two factors. \(-9 \times (-3) = 27\); \(27 \times (-2) = \mathbf{-54}\).

C3. A student computed \(-56 \div (-8)\) and got \(-7\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

Same signs should give a positive quotient. Correct: \(-56 \div (-8) = \mathbf{7}\).

C4. A student computed \(-12 + (-15)\) and got \(3\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

The student subtracted instead of adding. Same signs add and keep the sign: \(-12 + (-15) = \mathbf{-27}\).

C5. A student computed \(8 - 20\) and got \(12\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

The student subtracted the smaller from the larger and dropped the sign. Correct: \(8 - 20 = \mathbf{-12}\).

Real-World Precision - Harder Tier

C1. A hot air balloon at \(1{,}200\) m descends at \(-85\) m/min for 6 minutes, then rises \(150\) m. What is its final altitude?

Show solution

\(-85 \times 6 = -510\). \(1200 - 510 = 690\). \(690 + 150 = \mathbf{840}\) m.

C2. A company's profit changed by \(-\$1{,}250\) each month for 4 months, then it gained back \(\$2{,}000\) in month 5. What was the net change over all 5 months?

Show solution

\(-1250 \times 4 = -5000\). \(-5000 + 2000 = \mathbf{-\$3{,}000}\).

C3. Three deep-sea dives reach \(-320\) m, \(-410\) m, and \(-275\) m. What is the average depth?

Show solution

\(-320 + (-410) + (-275) = -1{,}005\). \(-1{,}005 \div 3 = \mathbf{-335}\) m.

C4. A stock opens at \(\$64\), drops \(\$3\) per hour for 5 hours, then a single correction adds \(\$22\). What is the closing price?

Show solution

\(-3 \times 5 = -15\). \(64 - 15 = 49\). \(49 + 22 = \mathbf{\$71}\).

C5. A scuba diver descends 18 m below the surface, then ascends 7 m, then descends another 12 m. What is the diver's final depth relative to the surface?

Show solution

\(-18 + 7 = -11\). \(-11 - 12 = \mathbf{-23}\) m.

Enrichment for Early Finishers

These are original problems, unrelated to the Mixed Practice assessment, safe to hand out to students who finish early.

C1. Find the missing integer: \(-14 + \underline{\hspace{1cm}} = -30\).

Show solution

\(-30 - (-14) = -30 + 14 = \mathbf{-16}\).

C2. Find the missing integer: \(\underline{\hspace{1cm}} - (-9) = 5\).

Show solution

If \(n + 9 = 5\), then \(n = 5 - 9 = \mathbf{-4}\).

C3. The product of three consecutive negative integers is \(-210\). What are the three integers?

Show solution

Try \(-5, -6, -7\): \((-5)(-6) = 30\), \(30 \times (-7) = -210\). The integers are \(\mathbf{-5}\), \(\mathbf{-6}\), and \(\mathbf{-7}\).

C4. A number decreased by 8 equals its own opposite. Find the number.

Show solution

\(n - 8 = -n \rightarrow 2n = 8 \rightarrow n = \mathbf{4}\). Check: \(4 - 8 = -4\), and the opposite of \(4\) is \(-4\). ✓

C5. Two integers are opposites. Their difference is 24. What are the two integers?

Show solution

Let the integers be \(a\) and \(-a\). \(a - (-a) = 2a = 24 \rightarrow a = 12\). The integers are \(\mathbf{12}\) and \(\mathbf{-12}\).
