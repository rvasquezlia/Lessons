Lesson Title: 1.1-1.3 Integers, Opposites, and Adding/Subtracting Integers

  • Whole numbers: \(0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\) (no negatives, no fractions).
  • Integers: \(\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\) - whole numbers and their opposites.
  • Positive numbers: greater than 0. Negative numbers: less than 0.
  • Opposite integers: two integers the same distance from 0 on opposite sides (e.g. \(5\) and \(-5\)).
  • Absolute value \(|a|\): the distance a number is from 0 on the number line - always \(0\) or positive.

Lesson 1.1: Relate Integers and Their Opposites
Every integer has an opposite the same distance from \(0\) but on the other side of the number line. The absolute value of a number is that distance - always \(0\) or positive, never negative.

Lesson 1.3: Add Integers
Same signs: add the absolute values, keep the common sign. Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger, and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

Lesson 1.4: Subtract Integers
Subtracting is adding the opposite: \(a - b = a + (-b)\). Rewrite the subtraction as addition of the opposite, then use the addition rules from Lesson 1.3.

Applications: Bank Balances & Temperature Changes
Multidisciplinary connection: Financial Literacy and Earth Science. Integer addition and subtraction model real changes to a quantity - money in an account, or temperature over a day.

Real-Life Scenario: Bank Balance

For this problem Deposits are positive changes, withdrawals are negative changes. Add them to the starting balance in order.

Malena's account starts at \(-\$85\) (she overdrew it). She deposits \(\$40\), then deposits \(\$25\), then withdraws \(\$30\).

Task: find her final balance.

Real-Life Scenario: Temperature Change

For this problem Find the noon temperature first (add the rise), then find the evening temperature (subtract the drop from the noon temperature).

The temperature at dawn was \(-4^\circ\text{C}\). By noon it rose \(11^\circ\text{C}\). By evening it had dropped \(15^\circ\text{C}\) from its noon value.

Task: find the noon temperature and the evening temperature.
