Key Vocabulary: Integers & Operations

Lessons 1.1–1.4 · CCSS.Math.Content.7.NS.A.1a–d & MP.7

Today we extend whole numbers to integers so we can describe quantities below zero — debts, elevations below sea level, temperatures below freezing — and learn how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide them.

Topic: Integers, Opposites, and Integer Operations (Lessons 1.1–1.4)

  • Whole numbers: \(0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\) (no negatives, no fractions).
  • Integers: \(\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\) — whole numbers and their opposites.
  • Positive numbers: greater than 0.   Negative numbers: less than 0.
  • Opposite integers: two integers the same distance from 0 on opposite sides (e.g. \(5\) and \(-5\)).
  • Absolute value \(|a|\): the distance a number is from 0 on the number line — always \(0\) or positive.
  • Adding integers: same signs → add and keep the sign; different signs → subtract and keep the sign of the larger absolute value.
  • Subtracting integers: add the opposite — \(a - b = a + (-b)\).
  • Multiplying / dividing integers: same signs → positive result; different signs → negative result.

Real-World Examples

  • Temperature: \(-5°C\) is 5 degrees below freezing. The opposite, \(+5°C\), is 5 degrees above freezing.
  • Bank balance: Earning $80 then spending $120 gives \(80 + (-120) = -40\) — you owe $40.
  • Elevation: The Dead Sea is about \(-430\text{ m}\) (430 m below sea level). \(|-430| = 430\text{ m}\) from sea level.
  • Stock price: A stock drops $3 each day for 4 days: \((-3) \times 4 = -12\) — a $12 total loss.
