Vocabulary You Need First
These are quick reminders from earlier topics. (Words like reciprocal, keep-change-flip, and equivalent forms are this topic's own vocabulary, so they're taught in its own Vocabulary & Literacy page, not here.)
Common Multiple

A number that two or more numbers all divide into evenly.

12 is a common multiple of 3 and 4
Like Denominators

Two or more fractions that already share the same denominator, so they're ready to add or subtract directly.

\(\frac{2}{7}\) and \(\frac{5}{7}\)
Unlike Denominators

Fractions with different denominators – they must be rewritten with a common denominator before you can add or subtract them.

\(\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\)
Fraction ↔ Decimal Recap
This topic mixes fractions and decimals in the same problem constantly, so switching between the two forms has to be fast and automatic.
Fraction → Decimal

Divide the numerator by the denominator.

Example: \(\frac{3}{8}\). \(3 \div 8 = 0.375\) (terminates). \(\frac{2}{3}\). \(2 \div 3 = 0.6666\ldots = 0.\overline{6}\) (repeats).

Decimal → Fraction

Write the decimal digits over the matching place-value power of 10, then simplify.

Example: \(0.35 = \frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}\) (divide both by the GCF, 5).

Adding & Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators
You can only add or subtract fractions once they share the same denominator – you're not allowed to combine "thirds" with "fourths" any more than you could combine 3 apples with 4 oranges and call it 7 of either.
The 3-Step Method
  1. Find a common denominator – a number both denominators divide into evenly (often their product, or their LCD if you know it).
  2. Rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction using that new denominator.
  3. Add or subtract the numerators only – the denominator stays the same.
Worked Example

\(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}\). Common denominator: 12. \(\frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12}\), \(\frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}\). Add numerators: \(\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}\).

\(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{2}\). Common denominator: 6. \(\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{6}\). Subtract: \(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{3}{6} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\) (simplify at the end).
