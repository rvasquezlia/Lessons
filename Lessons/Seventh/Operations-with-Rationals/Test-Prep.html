Concept Review: Every Rule in One Place
What to do on this tab Skim the reference boxes below. If a rule feels shaky, review your notebook copy of the worked examples before you continue. Then answer the 4 Check Your Understanding problems.

Rational numbers are any numbers that can be written as a fraction of integers. The same-sign/different-sign rules from Integers apply the same way, whatever form the numbers take.

1. Add/Subtract
  • Subtraction? → Keep-Change-Change first.
  • Fractions → Butterfly Method for a common denominator. Decimals → line up the decimal points.
  • Same signs → add, keep the sign. Different signs → subtract smaller from larger, keep the sign of the larger.
2. Multiply & Divide
  • Dividing fractions? → Keep-Change-Flip first.
  • Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative.
3. Switching Forms & Multi-Step
  • Mixed fraction and decimal? → convert one first (never changes the value or sign).
  • Multi-step → parentheses, then ×/÷ left to right, then +/- left to right - re-ask "same or different signs?" at every step.

Error Analysis: Spot the Mistake
What to do on this tab Even strong students make mistakes like these. For each problem, name the specific error, the correct rule, and the right answer. This tab is quiz-style - you submit once, and your work is reviewed from your printed report rather than revealed on screen.
Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Adding or subtracting fractions without a common denominator - the numerators can't combine until the denominators match.
  • Forgetting that same signs give a positive product or quotient, even when both numbers are fractions or decimals less than 1.
  • Dividing fractions by multiplying without flipping the second fraction first (skipping Keep-Change-Flip).
  • Converting a fraction to a decimal incorrectly and carrying that error through the rest of the problem.
