The Sign Ladder (Addition & Subtraction)
Whenever you add or subtract signed numbers - integers, decimals, or fractions - ask yourself the same questions in the same order. If the problem is subtraction, there's one setup question first.
Setup Question (subtraction only): Keep-Change-Change
#QuestionWhat it's asking
0Is this addition or subtraction?If it's already addition, skip straight to Question 1.
0aWhat do I keep?The first number, exactly as written.
0bWhat do I change (the operation)?Subtraction becomes addition.
0cWhat do I change (the sign)?The second number's sign flips to its opposite.

Once you've rewritten it, the whole problem is addition - move on to Question 1.

The Sign Ladder (repeat for every addition)
#QuestionWhat it's asking
1What operation do we have?By now it should be addition (rewrite subtraction first if it isn't).
2How are the signs - same or different?Compare the sign of each number being added.
3What do I do with the numbers?Same signs: add the absolute values. Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger.
4What sign does the answer get?Same signs: keep the common sign. Different signs: keep the sign of whichever number has the greater absolute value.
Fractions and decimals need one extra setup step before Question 1 can start: fractions need a common denominator, and mixed forms (a fraction next to a decimal) need to be converted to the same form first. Multi-step problems just mean you climb the Sign Ladder more than once - solve the first two numbers completely, then run the whole ladder again on that result and the next number.
The Classify Ladder (Rational Number Test Prep)
Test #2 also asks you to classify numbers, not just compute with them. Two short ladders cover every classification question you'll see.
Terminating vs. Repeating Decimal
#QuestionWhat it's asking
1Is the fraction in lowest terms?Simplify first if it isn't - the denominator has to be fully reduced.
2What are the prime factors of the denominator?Break the denominator down completely.
3Are the only prime factors 2 and/or 5?Yes → the decimal terminates. No (any other prime factor, like 3 or 7 or 11) → it repeats.
Rational vs. Irrational
#QuestionWhat it's asking
1Can I simplify it first?A radical like \(\sqrt{16}\) might simplify to a whole number (\(\sqrt{16}=4\)) before you classify it - don't judge a number by its "scary" appearance.
2Can it be written as a fraction \(\dfrac{a}{b}\) of two integers?Every integer, fraction, terminating decimal, and repeating decimal can - that makes it rational.
3Does its decimal form go on forever with no repeating pattern?If yes (like \(\pi\) or \(\sqrt{2}\)), it's irrational - it cannot be written as a fraction of integers.
Ten Worked Examples (Test-Style)
These ten examples follow the same order and mix of question types as Test #2 - 3 classification/rewrite questions, 5 computations, and 2 word problems - just with different numbers, so you can practice the exact moves you'll need.
1. Does \(\dfrac{5}{12}\) convert to a terminating or repeating decimal? Classify
Round 1
Is the fraction in lowest terms?Yes - \(\gcd(5,12)=1\)
What are the prime factors of the denominator?\(12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3\)
Are the only prime factors 2 and/or 5?No - there's a factor of 3
\(\dfrac{5}{12} = 0.41\overline{6}\)
Repeating decimal
Classified? Yes.
2. Which addition expression is equivalent to \(-9.3 - (-4.7)\)? Rewrite & Solve
Round 1 - Keep-Change-Change
What operation do I see?Subtraction
What do I keep?\(-9.3\)
What do I change (the operation)?Subtraction → addition
What do I change (the sign)?\(-4.7\) becomes \(+4.7\)
\(-9.3 - (-4.7) = -9.3 + 4.7\)
Rewritten as addition? Yes. Keep going - see next step.
Round 2 - Sign Ladder
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(9.3 - 4.7 = 4.6\)
Which number has the greater absolute value?\(-9.3\) (\(|-9.3| = 9.3 > |4.7| = 4.7\))
What sign does the answer get?Negative
\(-9.3 + 4.7 = -4.6\)
Solved? Yes.
3. Is \(\sqrt{16}\) rational or irrational? Classify
Round 1
Can I simplify it first?Yes - \(\sqrt{16} = 4\)
Can it be written as a fraction of integers?Yes - \(4 = \dfrac{4}{1}\)
\(\sqrt{16} = 4 = \dfrac{4}{1}\)
Rational
Classified? Yes - don't be fooled by the radical sign; simplify first.
4. \(-\dfrac{5}{8} + 0.75\) Addition, Mixed Forms
Round 1 - Match the Forms
Are both numbers in the same form?No - one's a fraction, one's a decimal
Which should I convert?\(-\dfrac{5}{8}\) to a decimal: \(-0.625\)
\(-\dfrac{5}{8} + 0.75 = -0.625 + 0.75\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(0.75 - 0.625 = 0.125\)
Which number has the greater absolute value?\(0.75\)
What sign does the answer get?Positive
\(-0.625 + 0.75 = 0.125\)
Solved? Yes.
5. \(-18.2 - (-6.5)\) Subtraction, Decimals
Round 1 - Keep-Change-Change
What do I keep?\(-18.2\)
What do I change (the operation)?Subtraction → addition
What do I change (the sign)?\(-6.5\) becomes \(+6.5\)
\(-18.2 - (-6.5) = -18.2 + 6.5\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(18.2 - 6.5 = 11.7\)
Which number has the greater absolute value?\(-18.2\)
What sign does the answer get?Negative
\(-18.2 + 6.5 = -11.7\)
Solved? Yes.
6. \(-\dfrac{3}{5} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)\) Addition, Fractions
Round 1 - Common Denominator
Do these fractions share a denominator?No - convert to tenths
What is \(-\dfrac{3}{5}\) in tenths?\(-\dfrac{6}{10}\)
\(-\dfrac{3}{5} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right) = -\dfrac{6}{10} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder
How are the signs?Same (both negative)
What do I do with the numbers?Add: \(\dfrac{6}{10} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{7}{10}\)
What sign does the answer get?The common sign - negative
\(-\dfrac{6}{10} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right) = -\dfrac{7}{10}\)
Solved? Yes.
7. \(15.6 - 8\dfrac{1}{4} + (-1.6)\) Multi-Step
Round 1 - Setup
Convert \(8\dfrac{1}{4}\) to a decimal.\(8.25\)
What operation comes first?Subtraction: \(15.6 - 8.25\) → Keep-Change-Change → \(15.6 + (-8.25)\)
\(15.6 - 8.25 + (-1.6) = 15.6 + (-8.25) + (-1.6)\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder (first pair)
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(15.6 - 8.25 = 7.35\)
What sign does the answer get?Positive (\(15.6\) has the greater absolute value)
\(15.6 + (-8.25) = 7.35\)
Fully solved? No - the \(+(-1.6)\) is still waiting. Keep going - see next step.
Round 3 - Sign Ladder (second pair)
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(7.35 - 1.6 = 5.75\)
What sign does the answer get?Positive (\(7.35\) has the greater absolute value)
\(7.35 + (-1.6) = 5.75\)
Fully solved? Yes.
8. \(0 - (-13.25)\) Special Case: Zero
Round 1 - Keep-Change-Change
What do I keep?\(0\)
What do I change (the operation)?Subtraction → addition
What do I change (the sign)?\(-13.25\) becomes \(+13.25\)
\(0 - (-13.25) = 0 + 13.25\)
Round 2 - Adding Zero
What is 0 plus any number?That same number, unchanged
\(0 + 13.25 = 13.25\)
Solved? Yes.
9. Word Problem: At sunrise, the temperature was \(-6.25\)°C. By noon it rose \(9\dfrac{1}{2}\)°C. During an evening storm it dropped \(4.8\)°C. What was the final temperature? Word Problem
Round 1 - Translate to an Expression
What's the starting value?\(-6.25\)
"Rose \(9\dfrac{1}{2}\)" means?Add \(9.5\)
"Dropped \(4.8\)" means?Subtract \(4.8\)
\(-6.25 + 9.5 - 4.8\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder (first pair)
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(9.5 - 6.25 = 3.25\)
What sign does the answer get?Positive (\(9.5\) has the greater absolute value)
\(-6.25 + 9.5 = 3.25\)
Fully solved? No - still need to subtract \(4.8\). Keep going - see next step.
Round 3 - Sign Ladder (second pair)
Rewrite the subtraction.\(3.25 - 4.8 = 3.25 + (-4.8)\)
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(4.8 - 3.25 = 1.55\)
What sign does the answer get?Negative (\(-4.8\) has the greater absolute value)
Final temperature: \(-1.55\)°C
Fully solved? Yes.
10. Word Problem: A submarine is at a depth of \(-18.6\) m. It descends another \(12\dfrac{3}{4}\) m to inspect a wreck, then ascends \(9.35\) m toward the surface. What is the submarine's final depth? Word Problem
Round 1 - Translate to an Expression
What's the starting value?\(-18.6\)
"Descends another \(12\dfrac{3}{4}\)" means?Add \(-12.75\) (descending makes it more negative)
"Ascends \(9.35\)" means?Add \(+9.35\)
\(-18.6 + (-12.75) + 9.35\)
Round 2 - Sign Ladder (first pair)
How are the signs?Same (both negative)
What do I do with the numbers?Add: \(18.6 + 12.75 = 31.35\)
What sign does the answer get?The common sign - negative
\(-18.6 + (-12.75) = -31.35\)
Fully solved? No - still need to add \(9.35\). Keep going - see next step.
Round 3 - Sign Ladder (second pair)
How are the signs?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(31.35 - 9.35 = 22.0\)
What sign does the answer get?Negative (\(-31.35\) has the greater absolute value)
Final depth: \(-22\) m
Fully solved? Yes.
