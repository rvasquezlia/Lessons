The Operation Ladder (Addition & Subtraction)
One ladder, four questions, every time - for integers, decimals, and fractions alike. Ask them in order, out loud if it helps.
The Operation Ladder
#QuestionWhat to do
1What operation do I have?Addition → go straight to Question 2. Subtraction → Keep-Change-Change first: keep the first number, change the operation to addition, change the second number's sign to its opposite. Now it's addition - continue to Question 2.
2How are the signs - same or different?Compare the sign of the two numbers now being added.
3What do I do with the numbers?Same signs: add the absolute values. Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger.
4What sign does the answer get?Same signs: keep the common sign. Different signs: keep the sign of whichever number has the greater absolute value.

Multi-step problem? Just climb this same ladder more than once - solve the first two numbers completely, then run the whole ladder again on that result and the next number.

Quick Reminder: Converting Between Decimals & Fractions
To convert...Do this
Fraction → decimalDivide the numerator by the denominator (long division).
Decimal → fractionWrite the digits over the matching place value - tenths → \(/10\), hundredths → \(/100\), thousandths → \(/1000\) - then simplify.
Mixed number → decimalConvert only the fraction part to a decimal, then add it to the whole number: \(8\dfrac{1}{4} = 8 + 0.25 = 8.25\).
Mixed number → fractionMultiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, keep the same denominator: \(8\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{8\times4+1}{4} = \dfrac{33}{4}\).
Fractions or Decimals? Choosing a Form for Mixed Problems

When one number is a fraction and the other is a decimal, you can't add or subtract them yet - convert them to the same form first.

  • Convert the fraction to a decimal when its denominator is "friendly" (only 2s and/or 5s: 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100...) - it will divide out evenly.
  • Convert the decimal to a fraction when the decimal repeats, or when the rest of the problem is already in fractions.
  • Not sure which is easier? Either direction works - just pick one and stay consistent for the whole problem.
Butterfly Method: Adding & Subtracting Fractions
  1. If it's subtraction, rewrite it as addition first (Keep-Change-Change).
  2. Cross-multiply each numerator by the other fraction's denominator - these are the two "wings."
  3. Multiply the two denominators straight down the middle for the new denominator.
  4. Apply the Operation Ladder to the two wing values - they're just two signed numbers now.
  5. Simplify the result to lowest terms.
The Classify Ladder - Prove It By Dividing
You only need one tool to answer every classification question on the test: do the division, and look at what happens.
Rational or Irrational? Just Divide.
#QuestionWhat to do
1Do I have a fraction?Divide it out: numerator ÷ denominator (long division). If it's a square root, simplify it first (\(\sqrt{16}=4\)) and treat that result the same way.
2Does the division terminate, or does it repeat?Terminates = the division stops, remainder becomes 0. Repeats = a block of digits comes back forever (mark it with a bar).
3So - rational or irrational?Terminates OR repeats → RATIONAL. Goes on forever with no repeating pattern → IRRATIONAL.

Good news: if you started with a fraction of two integers, the division can only ever terminate or repeat - it is always rational. Irrational numbers (like \(\pi\) or \(\sqrt{2}\)) only show up when you can't write the number as a fraction to begin with.

What do I do first?Divide: \(5 \div 12\) (long division)

Step 2: \(20 \div 12\)?1, remainder 8 (bring down a 0 → 80)
Step 3: \(80 \div 12\)?6, remainder 8 (bring down a 0 → 80 again - the remainder repeated!)
\(5 \div 12 = 0.41\overline{6}\)
Terminates or repeats?Repeats - remainder 8 keeps coming back, so the 6 repeats forever
Rational or irrational?Rational
Repeating decimal → Rational
Classified? Yes.
2. Which addition expression is equivalent to \(-9.3 - (-4.7)\)? Subtraction, Decimals
Round 1 - Operation Ladder
What operation do I have?Subtraction
Keep-Change-Change:Keep \(-9.3\), change to addition, change \(-4.7\) to \(+4.7\)
\(-9.3 - (-4.7) = -9.3 + 4.7\)
How are the signs now?Different
What do I do with the numbers?Subtract: \(9.3 - 4.7 = 4.6\)
What sign does the answer get?Negative (\(-9.3\) has the greater absolute value)
\(-9.3 + 4.7 = -4.6\)
Solved? Yes.
3. Is \(\sqrt{16}\) rational or irrational? Classify - Divide It Out
Round 1 - Prove It By Dividing
Can I simplify this first?Yes - \(\sqrt{16} = 4\)
Now divide it out (or is it already exact)?\(4 = 4 \div 1 = 4.0\) exactly - the division terminates immediately
Terminates or repeats?Terminates
Rational or irrational?Rational
\(\sqrt{16} = 4 = 4.0\)
Rational
Classified? Yes - don't be fooled by the radical sign; simplify first.
4. \(-\dfrac{5}{8} + 0.75\) Addition, Mixed Forms
Round 1 - Setup: Fractions or Decimals?
What operation do I have?Addition
Are both numbers in the same form?No - one's a fraction, one's a decimal

6. \(-\dfrac{3}{5} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right)\) Addition, Fractions (Butterfly)
Round 1 - Setup: Butterfly Method
What operation do I have?Addition
Cross-multiply the numerators (the wings):\(3 \times 10 = 30\) and \(5 \times 1 = 5\)
Multiply the denominators straight down the middle:\(5 \times 10 = 50\)
\(-\dfrac{3}{5} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{10}\right) = -\dfrac{30}{50} + \left(-\dfrac{5}{50}\right)\)
Round 2 - Operation Ladder (on the wings)
How are the signs?Same (both negative)
What do I do with the numbers?Add the wings: \(30 + 5 = 35\)
What sign does the answer get?The common sign - negative
Can I simplify?Yes - divide by the GCF, 5
\(-\dfrac{30}{50} + \left(-\dfrac{5}{50}\right) = -\dfrac{35}{50} = -\dfrac{7}{10}\)
Solved? Yes.

8. \(0 - (-13.25)\) Special Case: Zero
