Adding & Subtracting Signed Fractions
The sign rule doesn't change: same signs → add the absolute values and keep the common sign; different signs → subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. The only new step is that the numbers are fractions, so before you can add or subtract, you need a common denominator.
1. Add/Subtract Fractions - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Step 1: If subtracting, use Keep-Change-Change to rewrite it as addition.
  • Step 2 (Butterfly Method): cross-multiply each numerator by the other fraction's denominator to get the two "wing" values, and multiply the denominators straight down the middle for the new denominator.
  • Step 3: Apply the same-sign / different-sign rule to the numerators, just like you would with integers.
  • Step 4: Simplify to lowest terms if possible.

Adding & Subtracting Signed Decimals
Same rule again: same signs → add and keep the sign; different signs → subtract and keep the sign of the larger absolute value. With decimals, the setup step is lining up the place value (the decimal points), the same way you would for a plain decimal problem - then apply the integer sign rule to decide the final sign.
2. Add/Subtract Decimals - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Step 1: If subtracting, use Keep-Change-Change to rewrite it as addition.
  • Step 2: Line up the decimal points, adding placeholder zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places.
  • Step 3: Apply the same-sign / different-sign rule to decide both the digits and the final sign.

Multiplying & Dividing Rational Numbers
Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative. This is the exact same rule from Integers, and it applies no matter what form the numbers are in. For fractions, multiply straight across (numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator) and simplify. To divide fractions, use Keep-Change-Flip: keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction to its reciprocal. For decimals, multiply or divide as usual, then attach the sign the rule gives you.
3. Multiply & Divide Rational Numbers - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Same Signs = Positive: \(\left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) \times \left(-\frac{2}{5}\right) = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}\). \(-2.8 \times (-1.5) = 4.2\).
  • Different Signs = Negative: \(-\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{9} = -\frac{10}{27}\). \(12.6 \div (-3) = -4.2\).
  • Dividing fractions: Keep-Change-Flip, then apply the sign rule to the result. \(\frac{5}{8} \div \left(-\frac{5}{6}\right) = \frac{5}{8} \times \left(-\frac{6}{5}\right) = -\frac{3}{4}\).

Switching Between Fractions & Decimals Mid-Problem
Sometimes a problem hands you one fraction and one decimal in the same expression. You don't need a special new rule for this - you just need to convert one form into the other before you apply the sign rule, so both numbers are in a form you can actually combine. Pick whichever direction makes the arithmetic easier: fractions like \(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}\) convert to clean decimals, while a repeating decimal is often easier left as a fraction.
4. Switching Forms - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Common fraction-decimal pairs to know by heart: \(\frac{1}{2}=0.5\), \(\frac{1}{4}=0.25\), \(\frac{3}{4}=0.75\), \(\frac{1}{8}=0.125\), \(\frac{1}{10}=0.1\), \(\frac{1}{5}=0.2\).
  • Converting doesn't change the sign. \(-\frac{3}{8}\) and \(-0.375\) are the same negative number, just written two ways.
  • Rule of thumb: convert to decimal when the fraction is one of the "clean" ones above; convert to fraction when the decimal repeats or the problem is otherwise built around fractions.

Multi-Step Expressions & Order of Operations
Once you're combining several signed rational numbers in one expression, order of operations still runs the show: parentheses first, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. Handle one operation at a time, decide its sign, and keep going - don't try to guess the final sign of the whole expression in your head.
5. Multi-Step Strategy - COPY INTO NOTEBOOK
  • Step 1: Work inside any parentheses first.
  • Step 2: Do all multiplication and division, left to right - converting forms first if that makes a step cleaner.
  • Step 3: Finish with addition and subtraction, left to right, applying the same-sign/different-sign rule at each step.
