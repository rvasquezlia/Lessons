Quick Refresher: Terms You'll Lean On
What to do on this tab Read and copy each term, then try the 3 checks below. 2 tries per question.
Numerator

The top number in a fraction - it tells you how many parts you have. In \(\frac{3}{4}\), the numerator is \(3\).

Denominator

The bottom number in a fraction - it tells you how many equal parts something is divided into. In \(\frac{3}{4}\), the denominator is \(4\).

Reciprocal

Flip numerator and denominator. \(\frac{2}{5} \rightarrow \frac{5}{2}\). Sign stays the same.

Least Common Denominator (LCD)

Smallest number both denominators divide into evenly.

Equivalent Forms

Same value, different look: \(\frac12 = 0.5\). Converting never changes value or sign.

Opposites work the same way: \(-\frac34\) and \(\frac34\) are the same distance from zero, opposite sides - just like \(-4\) and \(4\).

Sign-Rule Vocabulary: Naming the Strategies
What to do on this tab Same strategy names as Integers, now for fractions and decimals. Copy them, then try the matching check.
Same Signs / Different Signs

First thing to check before any operation - both positive/negative, or one of each?

Keep-Change-Change

Subtraction → addition: keep the first number, change − to +, flip the second's sign. \(-\frac34-\left(-\frac12\right) \rightarrow -\frac34+\frac12\).

Keep-Change-Flip

Dividing fractions: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second to its reciprocal.

Simplify / Lowest Terms

Divide numerator and denominator by their GCF. \(\frac68 \rightarrow \frac34\).
