Topic Overview: Operations with Rational Numbers
Synthesis topic, following Integers and Rational Numbers. Students already have the same-sign/different-sign rules from Integers, and they already know how to compute with plain fractions and decimals from Rational Numbers. This topic is specifically the layer where those two skills meet: applying the integer sign rules to signed fractions and decimals, together, including converting between forms mid-problem when that makes a computation easier. It runs as six standalone pages - Vocabulary & Literacy, Explanation, Practice Set, and Word Problems in roughly that order, then Test Prep to review before an assessment. The pages don't link to each other, so share each one directly - a Google Classroom post per page works well. Calculators are not permitted this quarter (Q1-Q2) - they return in Q3.

Standards & Objectives

CCSS.Math.Content.7.NS.A.1, 7.NS.A.1a-d, 7.NS.A.2, 7.NS.A.2a-c, 7.NS.A.3 & MP.1, MP.2, MP.3, MP.5, MP.7, MP.8.

  • Add and subtract signed fractions and decimals using the same-sign/different-sign rules from Integers.
  • Multiply and divide signed fractions and decimals, including using Keep-Change-Flip for fraction division.
  • Convert between fraction and decimal form mid-problem to simplify a computation.
  • Evaluate multi-step expressions mixing several signed rational numbers, applying order of operations.
  • Apply these skills to real-world contexts: temperature, elevation/depth, rate of change, and banking.
Multidisciplinary Connections
  • Geography & Earth Science: elevation, depth, temperature change.
  • Financial Literacy: bank balances, deposits/withdrawals/fees with cents and fractional shares.
  • Physical Science: average rate of change (cooling rates, descent rates).
  • Critical Thinking: error analysis, choosing the more efficient number form for a given step.
Materials Needed
  • One device per student for all six pages.

Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Adding or subtracting fractions without a common denominator first.
  • Assuming a same-sign product or quotient is negative just because both numbers are fractions or decimals less than 1 in absolute value.
  • Dividing fractions by multiplying straight across instead of flipping the second fraction (skipping Keep-Change-Flip).
  • Converting a fraction to a decimal (or vice versa) incorrectly, which then carries an error through the rest of a multi-step problem.
  • Losing track of the sign when several operations are chained together in one expression.

Metacognition & Discussion

Reflection prompts and small-group discussion questions to use alongside any of the seven pages - none of this is graded content on a student page, it's for you to drop into a warm-up, an exit ticket, or a mid-lesson pause.

Reflection Prompts (use as an exit ticket or journal stem)
  • "Before I added or subtracted two fractions today, did I check that they had the same denominator first, or did I combine them too soon?"
  • "A fraction or decimal smaller than 1 can still combine with another negative to make a POSITIVE answer. Where today did I almost forget that same-signs rule applies no matter how small the numbers look?"
  • "Did I remember Keep-Change-Flip every time I divided fractions, or did I catch myself multiplying straight across by mistake?"
  • "When I switched a fraction to a decimal (or back) in the middle of a problem, how did I check that my conversion was actually correct before using it?"
  • "In a problem with more than one operation, where was I most likely to lose track of a negative sign, and what could I do differently next time?"

Scaffolded Support Problems

For a student who is still guessing rather than reasoning through the sign rule or Keep-Change-Flip. Read each hint aloud before they attempt the problem - unlike the graded Practice Set items, the scaffolding is meant to be visible here, not discovered. Formats below match how Practice Set actually grades each skill: an add/subtract-fractions or multiply/divide-fractions answer is graded as an exact fraction there (Tabs 1 and 3), never a decimal.

ProblemHint to giveAnswer
1. \(-\frac{3}{8}+\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)\)Same signs or different signs? Both negative - so find the common denominator first (LCD of 8 and 4 is 8: rewrite \(-\frac{1}{4}\) as \(-\frac{2}{8}\)), then add the numerators and keep the negative sign.\(\mathbf{-\frac{5}{8}}\)
2. \(\frac{7}{12}-\frac{5}{6}\)Rewrite the subtraction as adding the opposite first: \(\frac{7}{12}+\left(-\frac{5}{6}\right)\). Then find the common denominator (12) before applying the sign rule - and don't forget to simplify at the end.\(\mathbf{-\frac{1}{4}}\) (from \(-\frac{3}{12}\))
3. \(-4.5\times0.6\)Different signs give a negative product. Multiply the digits ignoring the signs first (\(4.5\times0.6=2.7\)), then attach the negative sign.\(\mathbf{-2.7}\)
4. \(-\frac{5}{8}\div\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\)Keep-Change-Flip first: keep \(-\frac{5}{8}\), change ÷ to ×, flip \(-\frac{2}{3}\) to \(-\frac{3}{2}\). Then ask: same signs or different signs? Both negative → positive product.\(\mathbf{\frac{15}{16}}\)
Extension / Challenge Problems

Stays within this unit's own standards (adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational numbers) while pushing past what's graded on any of the seven pages - more terms chained together than any single Practice Set or Word Problems item asks for.

ProblemAnswer / Notes
1. Evaluate \(-\frac{3}{4}+0.6-\frac{5}{8}\).Three terms mixing fraction and decimal form, one more term than anything graded on Practice Set's or Explanation's own "switching forms" tabs. Convert everything to a common form first (LCD 40, or decimal): \(\mathbf{-\frac{31}{40}=-0.775}\).
2. Evaluate \(-\frac{7}{12}\div\frac{5}{6}\times(-1.5)\).Chains a division and a multiplication in one expression - existing pages never chain more than one multiply/divide step together. Keep-Change-Flip the division first (\(-\frac{7}{12}\div\frac{5}{6}=-\frac{7}{10}\)), then multiply by \(-1.5\): \(\mathbf{\frac{21}{20}=1.05}\).
3. A weather balloon starts at an altitude of \(\frac{3}{4}\) km. It rises at a steady rate of \(0.15\) km every \(2\) minutes for \(8\) minutes, then drops \(0.2\) km due to wind. What is its final altitude, in km?Four chained operations (find a rate, project it forward, add, subtract) - more steps than any single Word Problems item. Rate: \(0.15\div2=0.075\) km/min; risen: \(0.075\times8=0.6\) km; running total: \(0.75+0.6=1.35\); final: \(1.35-0.2=\mathbf{1.15}\) km.

Add & Subtract Rational Numbers - Harder Tier

C1. Evaluate \(-\frac{5}{6}+\frac{3}{4}-\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)\).

Show solution

LCD of 6, 4, 3 is 12: \(-\frac{10}{12}+\frac{9}{12}+\frac{4}{12}\). Add left to right: \(-\frac{10}{12}+\frac{9}{12}=-\frac{1}{12}\); \(-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{4}{12}=\mathbf{\frac{3}{12}=\frac{1}{4}}=0.25\).

C2. Evaluate \(-6.4+2.75-(-1.9)\).

Show solution

\(-6.4+2.75=-3.65\). \(-3.65-(-1.9)=-3.65+1.9=\mathbf{-1.75}\).

C3. A diver at \(-14.25\) ft rises \(\frac{1}{2}\) of \(9\) ft, then descends \(3.5\) ft. What is her final depth?

Show solution

\(\frac{1}{2}\times9=4.5\). \(-14.25+4.5=-9.75\). \(-9.75-3.5=\mathbf{-13.25}\) ft.

C4. Find the missing number: \(-\frac{3}{4}+\underline{\hspace{1cm}}=\frac{1}{2}\).

Show solution

\(\frac{1}{2}-\left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{4}=\frac{2}{4}+\frac{3}{4}=\mathbf{\frac{5}{4}=1.25}\).

C5. Which has the greater value: \(-\frac{7}{8}\) or \(-0.9\)?

Show solution

\(-\frac{7}{8}=-0.875\). Since \(-0.875>-0.9\) (it's closer to zero), \(\mathbf{-\frac{7}{8}}\) is greater.

Multiply & Divide Rational Numbers - Harder Tier

C1. Evaluate \(-\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{9}{10}\div\left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)\).

Show solution

\(-\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{9}{10}=-\frac{18}{30}=-\frac{3}{5}\) (different signs, negative). \(-\frac{3}{5}\div\left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)=-\frac{3}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{3}\right)=\mathbf{1}\) (same signs, positive).

C2. Evaluate \(-4.5\times2\div(-0.9)\).

Show solution

\(-4.5\times2=-9\) (different signs, negative). \(-9\div(-0.9)=\mathbf{10}\) (same signs, positive).

C3. A tide gauge shows the water level dropping \(\frac{3}{8}\) inch every 15 minutes for 2 hours (8 fifteen-minute intervals). What is the total change?

Show solution

\(-\frac{3}{8}\times8=\mathbf{-3}\) in.

C4. Find two numbers whose product is \(-2.4\) and whose sum is \(-0.8\).

Show solution

Try factor pairs of \(2.4\) with opposite signs: \(-2\) and \(1.2\) give \((-2)(1.2)=-2.4\) and \(-2+1.2=-0.8\). The numbers are \(\mathbf{-2}\) and \(\mathbf{1.2}\).

C5. Evaluate \(\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 \times (-8)\).

Show solution

\(\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^2=\frac{1}{4}\) (squaring a negative gives a positive). \(\frac{1}{4}\times(-8)=\mathbf{-2}\).

Converting Forms & Multi-Step - Harder Tier

C1. Evaluate \(0.6+\frac{1}{3}\times(-1.5)\).

Show solution

\(\frac{1}{3}\times(-1.5)=-0.5\). \(0.6+(-0.5)=\mathbf{0.1}\).

C2. Evaluate \(\left(\frac{3}{4}-1.2\right)\div\left(-\frac{3}{10}\right)\).

Show solution

\(\frac{3}{4}-1.2=0.75-1.2=-0.45\). \(-0.45\div(-0.3)=\mathbf{1.5}\).

C3. A stock's value is \(\$36.50\). It drops by \(\frac{1}{5}\) of its value, then rises \(\$2.10\). What is its new value?

Show solution

\(\frac{1}{5}\times36.50=7.30\). \(36.50-7.30=29.20\). \(29.20+2.10=\mathbf{\$31.30}\).

C4. Evaluate \(-\frac{5}{8}+0.75\times\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\).

Show solution

\(0.75\times\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)=-0.5\). \(-\frac{5}{8}=-0.625\). \(-0.625+(-0.5)=\mathbf{-1.125}\).

C5. A submarine at \(-45.5\) m rises \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the distance to the surface (treat the surface as \(0\)), then descends \(3.25\) m. What is its final depth?

Show solution

\(\frac{1}{4}\times45.5=11.375\). \(-45.5+11.375=-34.125\). \(-34.125-3.25=\mathbf{-37.375}\) m.
