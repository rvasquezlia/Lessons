  • Whole Numbers: the numbers used for counting, starting from zero (\(0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\)). They don't include fractions, decimals, or negatives.
  • Integers: all the whole numbers, their negative counterparts, and zero (\(\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\)). Every whole number is an integer, but not every integer is a whole number.
  • Positive Numbers: any number greater than zero - to the right of zero on a number line.
  • Negative Numbers: any number less than zero - to the left of zero on a number line.
  • Opposites: two numbers the same distance from zero but in opposite directions. \(5\) and \(-5\) are opposites. The opposite of \(0\) is \(0\).
  • Absolute Value \(|x|\): a number's distance from zero, always non-negative. \(|5| = 5\) and \(|-5| = 5\).
Visualizing Opposites: \(-4\) and \(4\) - notice that both are exactly 4 units away from zero, just in opposite directions.

Integer Operations Reference Guide
What to do on this tab Copy the rules into your notebook, study the visual example, then answer the two Check Your Understanding problems.

Integers are whole numbers and their opposites (\(\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\)). This guide provides the essential rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers.

1. Addition Rules
  • Same Signs: Add the absolute values and keep the common sign. \(5+3=8\)    \(-5+(-3)=-8\)
  • Different Signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger. Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. \(-7+4=-3\) (subtract \(7-4=3\); keep negative because \(|-7|>|4|\)). \(10+(-2)=8\) (subtract \(10-2=8\); keep positive because \(|10|>|-2|\)).
Visualizing Addition: \(-3 + 5 = 2\) - starting at 0, move left 3 units, then right 5 units to land at 2.
2. Subtraction Rules: "Keep-Change-Change"

To subtract an integer, add its opposite. Follow these three steps to rewrite the problem as addition:

  1. Keep the first number exactly as it is.
  2. Change the subtraction sign to an addition sign.
  3. Change the sign of the second number to its opposite.

Example: \(4 - (-3)\): Keep the 4, Change \(-\) to \(+\), Change \(-3\) to \(3\) → \(4 + 3 = \mathbf{7}\).

Example: \(-2 - 5\): Keep \(-2\), Change \(-\) to \(+\), Change \(5\) to \(-5\) → \(-2 + (-5) = \mathbf{-7}\).

3. Multiplication & Division Sign Laws
  • Same Signs = Positive: \((+)\times(+)=(+)\), \((-)\times(-)=(+)\). Examples: \(6\times2=12\), \(-4\times(-3)=12\), \(-20\div(-5)=4\).
  • Different Signs = Negative: \((+)\times(-)=(-)\), \((-)\times(+)=(-)\). Examples: \(8\times(-2)=-16\), \(-9\times3=-27\), \(15\div(-3)=-5\).

Remember the Sign Laws

Multiplying or dividing: Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative.
Adding: Same signs → add and keep the sign. Different signs → subtract and keep the sign of the larger absolute value.

Clue Words Reference

Positive (+): Deposit, Gain, Above Sea Level, Ascend, Credit, Rise.
Negative (-): Withdraw, Loss, Below Sea Level, Descend, Debt, Debit.
Multiplication Clues: Rate, Per, Each.    Division Clues: Shared, Average, Per.

1. Addition Section

Guided Example (Temperature): It was \(-8\)°F and rose \(12\)°F. Expression: \(-8 + 12 = 4\). The new temperature is \(4\)°F.

2. Subtraction Section

Guided Example (Elevation): A bird is 20ft above sea level (\(+20\)) and dives 25ft down (\(-25\)). Expression: \(20 - 25 = -5\). Result: 5ft below sea level.

3. Multiplication Section

Guided Example (Stock Market): A stock drops \$3 each day for 4 days. Expression: \(-3 \times 4\). The total change is \(-\$12\).

4. Division Section

Guided Example (Shared Debt): Four friends together owe \$80 for a lost library book, shared equally. Expression: \(-80 \div 4\). Each person owes \$20 (\(-\$20\)).
