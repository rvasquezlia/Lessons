What to do on this tab Pick a rule below to review it, copy it into your notebook, then answer the two Check Your Understanding problems.
Addition Rules
  • Same signs: add the absolute values and keep the common sign. \(-5 + (-3) = -8\)
  • Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger; keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. \(-10 + 7 = -3\)
Subtraction Rule: Keep-Change-Change

Keep the first number, change subtraction to addition, change the sign of the second number.

Example: \(4 - (-9) \rightarrow 4 + 9 = \mathbf{13}\)

Multiplication & Division Sign Laws
  • Same signs: the result is always positive. \(-4 \times (-5) = 20\)
  • Different signs: the result is always negative. \(-20 \div 5 = -4\)

Ana says \(-10 - 4 = -6\), reasoning that \(10 - 4 = 6\) and the sign is negative. Is she correct?

Ana treated this as a same-signs problem, but it isn't one - there's no second negative to combine with. Using Keep-Change-Change: \(-10 - 4 \rightarrow -10 + (-4)\). Now the signs are the same, so add the absolute values and keep the negative sign: \(10 + 4 = 14\), giving \(-14\). She mixed up "same signs add" with treating the whole expression as if the 4 were already negative-and-being-subtracted, rather than converting the operation first.
