Foundational Concept: Same-Size Pieces
The One Rule That Runs Everything: you can only add or subtract fractions when the pieces are the same size - the same denominator. If the denominators don't match, rename the fractions first.
COPY INTO MATH NOTEBOOK

Lesson Title: Operations with Fractions - Addition & Subtraction

  • Like Denominators: add or subtract the numerators, keep the denominator, simplify.
  • Unlike Denominators: Butterfly Method - cross-multiply for the wings, multiply the denominators for the new one, then follow the like-denominator rule.
  • Mixed Numbers: combine the whole numbers and fraction parts separately - regroup if the fraction you're subtracting is bigger, or if the fraction sum is improper.
  • Whole Number + Fraction: adding just attaches the fraction; subtracting regroups the whole as a fraction first.

Estimation Strategy: Benchmark Fractions

Estimate first with a benchmark, then calculate exactly. If the exact answer isn't close to your estimate, something's off.

Common benchmarks: \(0\), \(\frac{1}{2}\), and \(1\).

Example: \(\frac{5}{9} + \frac{4}{7}\) - both fractions are close to \(\frac{1}{2}\), so the estimate is about \(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1\). The exact answer, \(\frac{71}{63} = 1\frac{8}{63}\), is very close to that estimate.

Adding & Subtracting Mixed Numbers
The Question Ladder
#Ask...Then...
1Same denominator on the fraction parts?No → Butterfly Method on the fraction parts first.
2Adding or subtracting the fraction parts?Adding: if the sum is improper, regroup - carry 1 into the whole number. Subtracting: if the fraction you have is smaller than the one being subtracted, regroup 1 whole into an extra fraction first.
3Combine the whole numbers.Add or subtract the whole-number parts, using any regrouped values.
4Is it simplified?Reduce to simplest form.

Whole Numbers Plus Fractions
The Question Ladder
#Ask...Then...
1Adding or subtracting a fraction and a whole number?Adding: just attach the fraction - nothing to regroup. Subtracting: regroup the whole number as (whole − 1) plus a fraction equal to 1 first.
2Whole number + mixed number?Combine with the whole-number part only - the fraction part doesn't change.
