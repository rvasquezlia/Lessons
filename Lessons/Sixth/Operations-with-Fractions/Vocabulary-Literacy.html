Word Bank: Operations with Fractions
What to do on this tab
  • Read every card below and copy the Word Bank into your math notebook.
  • You'll see these words again in Explanation, Practice Set, Word Problems, and Test Prep - knowing them now makes everything else easier.
Adding and subtracting fractions is all about matching piece sizes - the denominator. Every term below connects back to that one idea.
Numerator

The top number in a fraction - how many equal parts you have.

In \(\frac{5}{8}\), 5 is the numerator.
Denominator

The bottom number in a fraction - how many equal parts the whole is split into.

In \(\frac{5}{8}\), 8 is the denominator.
Like Denominators

Fractions whose denominators are the same - ready to add or subtract right away.

\(\frac{2}{7}\) and \(\frac{4}{7}\) have like denominators.
Unlike Denominators

Fractions whose denominators are different - they need a common denominator before adding or subtracting.

\(\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\) have unlike denominators.
Butterfly Method

A way to combine fractions with unlike denominators: cross-multiply for the wings, multiply the denominators for the new one.

\(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}\) → wings \(6\) and \(4\), new denominator \(24\).
Wings

The two cross-products in the Butterfly Method - each numerator times the denominator across from it.

For \(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}\): \(1\times6=6\) and \(4\times1=4\).
Equivalent Fractions

Fractions that name the same amount, found by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number.

\(\frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12}\)
Simplify (Reduce)

To rewrite a fraction in a smaller, equivalent form - all the way to simplest form when finishing a problem.

\(\frac{6}{8}\) simplifies to \(\frac{3}{4}\).
Mixed Number

A whole number and a fraction written together.

\(3\frac{2}{5}\)
Improper Fraction

A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator.

\(\frac{17}{5}\) is the same amount as \(3\frac{2}{5}\).
Regroup (Fraction Subtraction)

When the fraction you're subtracting is bigger than the fraction you have, "borrow" 1 whole from the whole-number part and rewrite it as an extra fraction.

\(5\frac{1}{4}\) regroups to \(4\frac{5}{4}\) before subtracting \(\frac{3}{4}\).
Benchmark Fraction

A familiar fraction like \(\frac{1}{2}\), \(\frac{1}{4}\), or \(1\), used to estimate a sum or difference before calculating exactly.

\(\frac{5}{9} + \frac{4}{7}\) is close to \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1\).
Sum

The answer to an addition problem.

The sum of \(\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\) is \(\frac{1}{2}\).
Difference

The answer to a subtraction problem.

The difference between \(\frac{3}{4}\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\) is \(\frac{1}{2}\).
Reasonableness

Whether an exact answer makes sense compared to an estimate using benchmark fractions.

If your exact answer isn't close to your benchmark estimate, recheck your common denominator.

Every word problem follows the same routine: estimate first with a benchmark fraction, then calculate the exact answer and compare. But first you need to know which operation the words are asking for - that's what these keywords are for.
COPY INTO MATH NOTEBOOK
Addition (+)
  • total, sum, combined, altogether, in all, increased by
Subtraction (−)
  • difference, how much more, how much left, remaining, decreased by, less than (turns the order around!)
The "Less Than" Turn-Around
  • "\(\frac{1}{4}\) less than \(\frac{3}{4}\)" means \(\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{4}\), not \(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{4}\).
  • "Than" reverses the order - always write the second amount first when you see "less than."
