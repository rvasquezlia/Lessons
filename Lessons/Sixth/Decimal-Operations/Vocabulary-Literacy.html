Word Bank: Decimal Operations
What to do on this tab Read every card below and copy the Word Bank into your math notebook. You'll see every one of these words again in Explanation, Practice Set, Word Problems, and Test Prep - knowing them now makes everything else in this topic easier.
Decimal Operations is all about place value - the value a digit has because of its position in a number. Every term below connects back to that one idea.
Decimal Point (\(\cdot\))

The dot that separates whole quantities (on the left) from fractional parts (on the right).

In \(14.9\), the decimal point sits between the ones and the tenths.
Place Value

The value a digit has because of where it sits in a number - each place is \(10\times\) the value of the place to its right.

In \(354.2\), the 3 represents 300 because it's in the hundreds place.
Tenths (\(0.1\))

\(\frac{1}{10}\)th of a whole - the first place to the right of the decimal point.

1 dime is \(\$0.10\), or one tenth of a dollar.
Hundredths (\(0.01\))

\(\frac{1}{100}\)th of a whole - the second place to the right of the decimal point.

1 penny is \(\$0.01\), or one hundredth of a dollar.
Thousandths (\(0.001\))

\(\frac{1}{1000}\)th of a whole - the third place to the right of the decimal point.

Metric lab measurements and sports split-times often use thousandths.
Standard Algorithm

The step-by-step written method for adding, subtracting, or multiplying that lines digits up in columns.

Lining up decimal points before adding is part of the standard algorithm for addition.
Regroup (Carry)

When a column's sum is \(10\) or more, write the ones digit in that column and carry \(1\) to the next column on the left.

In \(12.00 + 4.85\), the ones column carries a \(1\) into the tens.
Regroup (Borrow)

When a column can't subtract without going negative, "borrow" \(1\) from the column to its left, striking through and rewriting that digit.

In \(20.00 - 13.45\), the hundredths column borrows all the way from the tens.
Partial Product

One piece of a multiplication problem, found by multiplying by a single digit of the second factor before adding all the pieces together.

In \(245 \times 32\), \(245 \times 2 = 490\) and \(245 \times 30 = 7350\) are the two partial products.
Decimal Places

The number of digits written to the right of the decimal point.

\(3.5\) has 1 decimal place; \(1.25\) has 2 decimal places.
Estimate

An approximate answer found by rounding, calculated before the exact answer as a reasonableness check.

Before computing \(14.25 + 8.7\) exactly, estimate \(14 + 9 = 23\).
Round

To replace a number with a nearby, simpler value based on the digit right after the target place.

\(14.86\) rounds to \(14.9\) (nearest tenth) because the hundredths digit, \(6\), is \(5\) or greater.
Sum

The answer to an addition problem.

The sum of \(3.5\) and \(1.25\) is \(4.75\).
Difference

The answer to a subtraction problem.

The difference between \(25.00\) and \(22.95\) is \(2.05\).
Product

The answer to a multiplication problem.

The product of \(3.5\) and \(1.25\) is \(4.375\).
Reasonableness

Whether an exact answer makes sense compared to the estimate - a check that catches decimal-placement mistakes.

If your exact answer isn't close to your estimate, you likely lined up digits or shifted the decimal incorrectly.

Reading a Decimal Out Loud

Say the whole-number part normally, say "and" for the decimal point, then read the decimal digits as if they were a whole number followed by the name of the smallest place shown.

Example: \(14.9\) is read "fourteen and nine tenths." \(3.05\) is read "three and five hundredths."

The "Less Than" Turn-Around

Why does the phrase "6.5 less than 10.75" result in the expression \(10.75 - 6.5\) instead of \(6.5 - 10.75\)? The word "than" reverses the numerical order compared to how it's written in the phrase - always write the second number first when you see "less than."
