Full Review: Adding and Subtracting Decimals
What to do on this tab Read the estimation strategy box, then click "Next Step" on Example 1 and Example 2 to review each worked problem. Try the practice problem yourself at the bottom - you get two tries before the method is shown.
When adding or subtracting decimals, the most important rule is to line up the decimal points. This ensures you are adding or subtracting digits with the same place value (tenths with tenths, ones with ones, etc.).
The Strategy: Estimate First!

Before calculating the exact answer, always round your numbers to the nearest whole number to find an estimate.

  • If your exact answer is close to your estimate, your decimal placement is likely correct.
  • If your exact answer is very different, you may have lined up the digits incorrectly.

Example 1: Adding Decimals (\(12.7 + 4.25\))

Estimate Check: \(12.7 \approx 13\), \(4.25 \approx 4\) → \(13 + 4 = 17\)

Example 2: Subtracting Decimals (\(8.4 - 2.76\))

Estimate Check: \(8.4 \approx 8\), \(2.76 \approx 3\) → \(8 - 3 = 5\)

Full Review: Multiplying Decimals
What to do on this tab Read the Golden Rule box, then click through the worked multiplication example step by step. Try the guided practice problem, then answer the Critical Thinking question.
Multiplying decimals is very similar to multiplying whole numbers. The biggest difference is knowing where to place the decimal point in your final answer. We use estimation to make sure our answer makes sense.
The Golden Rule

When multiplying decimals, you do not need to line up the decimal points like you do for addition and subtraction. Instead, you count the total number of decimal places in the factors to place the decimal in your product.

Worked Example: \(3.2 \times 4.8\)

Estimate Check: \(3.2 \approx 3\), \(4.8 \approx 5\) → \(3 \times 5 = 15\)
