How to Run This Jigsaw

Every group solves the same kind of decimal problems - the same skills, different real-world stories. Each theme's card has exactly 6 problems, all genuine word-problem scenarios (no bare computation filler). The page does not time or advance itself; pacing and circulating are teacher-led.

  • Before class: assign each table a theme (Medicine, Market, Engineering, or Arts) and print/hand out that theme's 6 problem cards from the Printables link above - the cards match the on-screen version exactly.
  • Tab 1: each student picks the same theme their table was assigned, confirms it (this locks - they cannot switch or peek at another theme's problems), then solves and submits all 6 on their own device.
  • Once a table finishes (mixing, ~15 min): send them to find someone from a different theme. They should ask what kind of problems that person solved and why precision mattered in that field - not check math, just listen and learn.
  • Tab 2: after mixing, each student picks which theme they talked to and writes 2-3 sentences on what they learned about that field's real-world use of decimals.
  • Exit ticket: students scan or print-to-PDF their completed physical card and upload it to Google Classroom, in addition to printing their digital progress page from Tab 3.
  • Optional whole-class close: ask one student per theme to share out loud what their group's real-world use case was, so every student hears all four before the bell.

Monday - Decimal Operations, Harder Tier

C1. Compute \(124.5 \times 0.036\).

Show solution

Multiply as whole numbers: \(1245 \times 36 = 44{,}820\). Total decimal places: \(1 + 3 = 4\). Shift 4 places left: \(44820 \rightarrow \mathbf{4.482}\).

C2. A lab tech mixes three solutions: \(12.075\) L, \(8.4\) L, and \(0.925\) L. She splits the total evenly into 4 containers. How many liters go in each container?

Show solution

Total \(= 12.075 + 8.4 + 0.925 = 21.4\) L. Divide evenly: \(21.4 \div 4 = \mathbf{5.35}\) L per container.

C3. Find the missing digits \(A\) and \(B\): \(A7.B \times 4 = 110.8\).

Show solution

\(A7.B = 110.8 \div 4 = 27.7\). So \(A = \mathbf{2}\) and \(B = \mathbf{7}\).

C4. Compute \(0.125 \times 64.8\).

Show solution

\(125 \times 648 = 81{,}000\). Total decimal places: \(3+1=4\). Shift: \(\mathbf{8.1}\).

C5. Compute \(54.72 \div 6\).

Show solution

\(54.72 \div 6 = \mathbf{9.12}\).

Tuesday - Multi-Step Word Problems, Harder Tier

C1. A caterer buys \(15.5\) lbs of chicken at \(\$3.40\)/lb and \(8.25\) lbs of beef at \(\$5.60\)/lb, then pays a \(\$12.50\) delivery fee. She splits the total cost evenly across 4 event budgets. How much does each budget pay?

Show solution

Chicken \(= 15.5 \times 3.40 = \$52.70\). Beef \(= 8.25 \times 5.60 = \$46.20\). Subtotal \(= 52.70+46.20=\$98.90\). Plus delivery: \(98.90+12.50=\$111.40\). Split 4 ways: \(111.40 \div 4 = \mathbf{\$27.85}\) per budget.

C2. A runner's 4 lap times are \(68.45\)s, \(71.2\)s, \(69.85\)s, and \(70.5\)s. What was her average lap time?

Show solution

Total \(= 68.45+71.2+69.85+70.5=280.0\)s. Average \(= 280.0 \div 4 = \mathbf{70.0}\)s per lap.

C3. A store buys 24 phone cases at \(\$4.25\) each and sells them at \(\$9.99\) each. What is the total profit?

Show solution

Cost \(= 24 \times 4.25 = \$102.00\). Revenue \(= 24 \times 9.99 = \$239.76\). Profit \(= 239.76-102.00 = \mathbf{\$137.76}\).

C4. Four friends split a \(\$68.80\) dinner bill evenly, then each leaves an additional \(\$3.25\) tip. How much does each friend pay in total?

Show solution

Bill share: \(68.80 \div 4 = \$17.20\). Plus tip: \(17.20+3.25 = \mathbf{\$20.45}\) per friend.

C5. A vendor buys 15 crates of fruit at \(\$8.40\) each and gets a \(\$12.50\) bulk discount off the total. What does the vendor pay?

Show solution

\(15 \times 8.40 = \$126.00\). Minus discount: \(126.00-12.50 = \mathbf{\$113.50}\).

Wednesday - Error Analysis & Health Science, Harder Tier

C1. A student computed \(23.4 - 5.68 + 1.2\) and got \(30.28\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

Mistake: the student added \(5.68\) instead of subtracting it. Correct: \(23.4-5.68=17.72\); \(17.72+1.2=\mathbf{18.92}\).

C2. A student eats \(1.5\) bars of Snack A (\(5.2\) g fat/bar) and \(2\) bars of Snack B (\(3.75\) g fat/bar). What is the total fat?

Show solution

Snack A: \(1.5 \times 5.2 = 7.8\) g. Snack B: \(2 \times 3.75 = 7.5\) g. Total \(= 7.8+7.5 = \mathbf{15.3}\) g.

C3. A student computed \(0.25 \times 0.4\) and got \(1.0\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

Mistake: \(0.25\) has 2 decimal places and \(0.4\) has 1, so the product needs 3 decimal places - the student only shifted 1. Correct: \(25 \times 4 = 100\), shift 3 places: \(\mathbf{0.100 = 0.1}\).

C4. A student computed \(9.6 \div 0.8\) and got \(1.2\). Find the mistake and the correct answer.

Show solution

Mistake: the student didn't shift the decimal point in both numbers before dividing. Correct: \(9.6 \div 0.8 = 96 \div 8 = \mathbf{12}\) (check: \(0.8 \times 12 = 9.6\)).

C5. A hiker eats 3 granola bars with \(6.4\) g of protein each, then gives away \(2.5\) g of protein worth of bar to a friend. How much protein did the hiker actually eat?

Show solution

\(3 \times 6.4 = 19.2\) g. Minus given away: \(19.2 - 2.5 = \mathbf{16.7}\) g.
