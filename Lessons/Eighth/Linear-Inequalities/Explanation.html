Inequality Symbols & Graphing Basics
An inequality compares two expressions instead of setting them equal. Every inequality has a solution set - usually infinitely many values - which is why we graph it on a number line instead of naming just one number.
SymbolMeaningReal-World PhraseCircle
\(>\)Greater thanMore than, exceedsOpen
\(<\)Less thanFewer than, belowOpen
\(\geq\)Greater than or equal toAt least, no less thanClosed (filled)
\(\leq\)Less than or equal toAt most, no more thanClosed (filled)
The Two Questions Every Graph Answers

1. What kind of circle? Open (\(<\) or \(>\)) means the boundary point itself is not a solution. Closed/filled (\(\leq\) or \(\geq\)) means it is a solution.
2. Which direction? "Greater than" points right (toward bigger numbers); "less than" points left (toward smaller numbers).

Worked Example: Graph \(x > 3\)

\(>\) is strict, so \(3\) is not included → open circle at \(3\). "Greater than" points right.

Worked Example: Graph \(x \leq -2\)

\(\leq\) includes the boundary → closed (filled) circle at \(-2\). "Less than" points left.

Worked Example: Graph \(x \geq -6\)

\(\geq\) includes the boundary → closed circle at \(-6\). "Greater than" points right.

Reading a Graph Backwards

Given a graph, work the two questions in reverse: an open circle means a strict symbol (\(<\) or \(>\)); a filled circle means \(\leq\) or \(\geq\). An arrow pointing left means "less than"; pointing right means "greater than." An open circle at \(-1\) with an arrow to the left graphs \(x < -1\).

One-Step and Two-Step Inequalities
Solving an inequality uses the exact same inverse-operations process as solving an equation. The only thing that changes is one extra question you ask on every single step.
The Question Ladder for Inequalities (repeat as many times as needed)
#QuestionWhat it's asking
1What's the variable I need to isolate?Name the target letter. It doesn't change round to round.
2What is preventing me from doing so, and what is it doing?Name whatever is attached to the variable right now, and whether it's adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing.
3What's the inverse operation to remove it?Name the opposite operation.
4Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?The one new question. If yes, the inequality symbol flips when you apply this step. If no - including every addition/subtraction step - it never flips.

Apply question 3's answer to both sides (flipping the symbol if question 4 was "yes"), simplify, and ask: "Is the variable now isolated?" If not, the whole ladder repeats on whatever is preventing it next.

1. \(x + 5 > 12\) One Step
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(5\), adding
What's the inverse operation?Subtraction
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips
\(x + 5 - 5 > 12 - 5\)
\(5 - 5\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(12 - 5 = 7\) on the right
\(x > 7\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
2. \(x - 6 \leq 3\) One Step
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(6\), subtracting
What's the inverse operation?Addition
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is addition, so the symbol never flips
\(x - 6 + 6 \leq 3 + 6\)
\(-6 + 6\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(3 + 6 = 9\) on the right
\(x \leq 9\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
3. \(3x < 21\) One Step
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(3\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(3\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - \(3\) is positive, so the symbol stays the same
\(\dfrac{3x}{3} < \dfrac{21}{3}\)
\(\dfrac{3x}{3} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{21}{3} = 7\) on the right
\(x < 7\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
4. \(3x - 4 < 11\) Two-Step
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(4\) subtracting, and \(3\) multiplying
Which one do I undo first?\(4\) subtracting - peel off addition/subtraction before multiplication/division
What's the inverse operation?Addition
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is addition, so the symbol never flips
\(3x - 4 + 4 < 11 + 4\)
\(-4 + 4\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(11 + 4 = 15\) on the right
\(3x < 15\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.

What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(3\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(3\)

\(\dfrac{3x}{3} < \dfrac{15}{3}\)
\(\dfrac{3x}{3} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{15}{3} = 5\) on the right
\(x < 5\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.

Which one do I undo first?\(7\) adding

\(2x + 7 - 7 \geq 15 - 7\)
\(7 - 7\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(15 - 7 = 8\) on the right
\(2x \geq 8\)

\(\dfrac{2x}{2} \geq \dfrac{8}{2}\)

\(x \geq 4\)

The Flip Rule: Multiplying or Dividing by a Negative
This is the one rule that makes inequalities different from equations. Whenever you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must flip the direction of the inequality symbol. Forgetting this single step is the most common mistake in this whole topic.
Why Does the Symbol Have to Flip?

Start with a true statement: \(4 < 9\). Multiply both sides by \(-1\) without changing the symbol: \(-4 < -9\). Is that true? No - \(-4\) is actually greater than \(-9\) on the number line. To keep the statement true, the symbol has to flip: \(-4 > -9\). Multiplying by a negative number reverses which side is "bigger," so the inequality symbol has to reverse too.

1. \(-2x > 8\) Flip Required
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(-2\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(-2\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?Yes - \(-2\) is negative, so the symbol flips
\(\dfrac{-2x}{-2} < \dfrac{8}{-2}\) (flipped from \(>\) to \(<\))
\(\dfrac{-2x}{-2} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{8}{-2} = -4\) on the right
\(x < -4\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
Check it: try \(x = -5\) (should work, since \(-5 < -4\)): \(-2(-5) = 10 > 8\). True! Try \(x = -3\) (should not work, since \(-3\) is not less than \(-4\)): \(-2(-3) = 6 > 8\)? False - correctly excluded.
2. \(-x \leq 5\) Flip Required
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(-1\), multiplying (\(-x\) means \(-1 \cdot x\))
What's the inverse operation?Multiply both sides by \(-1\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?Yes - \(-1\) is negative, so the symbol flips
\(-1(-x) \geq -1(5)\) (flipped from \(\leq\) to \(\geq\))
\(-1 \times -x = x\) on the left; \(-1 \times 5 = -5\) on the right
\(x \geq -5\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
3. \(-3x + 4 > 19\) Flip Required
Round 1
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(4\) adding, and \(-3\) multiplying
Which one do I undo first?\(4\) adding
What's the inverse operation?Subtraction
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips here
\(-3x + 4 - 4 > 19 - 4\)
\(4 - 4\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(19 - 4 = 15\) on the right
\(-3x > 15\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.
Round 2
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(-3\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(-3\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?Yes - \(-3\) is negative, so the symbol flips
\(\dfrac{-3x}{-3} < \dfrac{15}{-3}\) (flipped from \(>\) to \(<\))
\(\dfrac{-3x}{-3} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{15}{-3} = -5\) on the right
\(x < -5\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
The Mistake to Avoid

Notice that Example 3's Round 1 asked the flip-check question too, and the answer was no - subtracting \(4\) never flips anything. The flip only ever happens on a multiplication/division-by-a-negative round. Ask the question on every round out of habit, even ones where you're sure the answer is no - that habit is exactly what prevents the single most common mistake in this topic.

Multi-Step Inequalities
Multi-step inequalities combine everything so far: distribute, combine like terms, collect the variable on one side, and isolate it - watching on every step for a multiplication or division by a negative.
1. \(2(x + 3) < 4x - 2\) Variable on Both Sides
Round 1 - Distribute First
Is there a quantity to distribute before I can even see what's preventing \(x\)?Yes - distribute the \(2\) into \((x+3)\)
\(2x + 6 < 4x - 2\)
\(2 \times x = 2x\), and \(2 \times 3 = 6\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No - it's on both sides now. Keep going - see next round.
Round 2
What's the variable I need to isolate?\(x\)
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(4x\) on the right side - move it over first
What's the inverse operation?Subtract \(4x\) from both sides
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips
\(2x + 6 - 4x < 4x - 2 - 4x\)
\(2x - 4x = -2x\) on the left; \(4x - 4x\) cancels to \(0\) on the right
\(-2x + 6 < -2\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.
Round 3
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(6\), adding
What's the inverse operation?Subtraction
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips
\(-2x + 6 - 6 < -2 - 6\)
\(6 - 6\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(-2 - 6 = -8\) on the right
\(-2x < -8\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.
Round 4
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(-2\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(-2\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?Yes - \(-2\) is negative, so the symbol flips
\(\dfrac{-2x}{-2} > \dfrac{-8}{-2}\) (flipped from \(<\) to \(>\))
\(\dfrac{-2x}{-2} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{-8}{-2} = 4\) on the right
\(x > 4\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.

Try It Yourself First: Distribute, Combine, and Flip

Problem: \(-3(x - 2) + 5 \leq 2x + 17\)

Work through the Question Ladder on paper first - distribute, combine like terms, then isolate \(x\) exactly like Example 1 above. Reveal one round at a time to check your own work.

Worked Applications: Real-World Inequalities
Word problems that use "at least," "at most," "more than," or "fewer than" are always inequalities, not equations - there's a limit or requirement, not one exact amount.
Worked Example: A Budget Limit ("At Most") Two-Step

A school trip costs \(\$45\) per student, plus a flat \(\$150\) bus fee. The total budget is at most \(\$600\). How many students, \(n\), can go?

Define: what does the variable represent?\(n\) = the number of students who go
Write it: what phrase tells me the symbol?"at most" means \(\leq\)
\(45n + 150 \leq 600\)
Round 1
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(150\), adding
What's the inverse operation?Subtraction
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips
\(45n + 150 - 150 \leq 600 - 150\)
\(150 - 150\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(600 - 150 = 450\) on the right
\(45n \leq 450\)
Is \(n\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.
Round 2
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(45\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(45\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - \(45\) is positive, so the symbol stays the same
\(\dfrac{45n}{45} \leq \dfrac{450}{45}\)
\(\dfrac{45n}{45} = n\) on the left; \(\dfrac{450}{45} = 10\) on the right
\(n \leq 10\)
Is \(n\) now isolated? Yes.
Interpret: at most \(\mathbf{10}\) students can go on the trip.
Worked Example: A Minimum Requirement ("At Least") Two-Step

You need at least 20 points to pass a class contest. You already have 12 points, and each additional assignment is worth 4 points. How many more assignments, \(x\), do you need?

Define: what does the variable represent?\(x\) = the number of additional assignments
Write it: what phrase tells me the symbol?"at least" means \(\geq\)
\(12 + 4x \geq 20\)
Round 1
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(12\), adding
What's the inverse operation?Subtraction
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - this is subtraction, so the symbol never flips
\(12 + 4x - 12 \geq 20 - 12\)
\(12 - 12\) cancels to \(0\) on the left; \(20 - 12 = 8\) on the right
\(4x \geq 8\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? No. Keep going - see next round.
Round 2
What is preventing me, and what is it doing?\(4\), multiplying
What's the inverse operation?Division by \(4\)
Am I multiplying or dividing by a negative?No - \(4\) is positive, so the symbol stays the same
\(\dfrac{4x}{4} \geq \dfrac{8}{4}\)
\(\dfrac{4x}{4} = x\) on the left; \(\dfrac{8}{4} = 2\) on the right
\(x \geq 2\)
Is \(x\) now isolated? Yes.
Interpret: you need at least \(\mathbf{2}\) more assignments.
The 4-Step Method for Word Problems
  • Define: what does the variable represent, and what are its units?
  • Write: find the limit/requirement phrase and translate it into an inequality.
  • Solve: isolate the variable, watching for the flip rule.
  • Interpret: answer the actual question in a full sentence, with units.
