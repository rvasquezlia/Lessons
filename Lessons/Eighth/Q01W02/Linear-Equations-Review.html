Linear Equation Fundamentals: Signs and Rules

Weekly Review · Lessons 1-2 & 1-3 · CCSS.Math.Content.8.EE.C.7

What to do on this tab Copy both reference tables into your notebook, read the Golden Rule box, then answer the two Check Your Understanding questions.

Before solving equations, you must master the rules of integers and the logic of keeping an equation balanced. Use this guide as your reference for the fundamental laws of algebra.

Part 1: Integer Operation Laws (Sign Rules)
OperationThe Rule
AdditionSame Signs: Add the absolute values and keep the sign. (\(-3+(-5)=-8\))
Different Signs: Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger; keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. (\(-10+4=-6\))
SubtractionAdd the Opposite: Change the subtraction sign to addition and change the sign of the second number, then follow addition rules. (\(5-(-2)\) becomes \(5+2=7\))
Multiplication & DivisionSame Signs: Always Positive (\(+\times+=+\) or \(-\times-=+\)). Different Signs: Always Negative (\(+\times-=-\)).
The Golden Rule of Equations: An equation is like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to one side of the equation, you MUST do to the other side. This maintains the equality while you work to isolate the variable.
Part 2: Inverse Operations

To solve an equation, you must "undo" the operations surrounding the variable using Inverse Operations.

If the equation shows...InverseAction to take on BOTH sides
Addition (\(+5\))↔Subtract 5
Subtraction (\(-8\))↔Add 8
Multiplication (\(3x\))↔Divide by 3
Division (\(\frac{x}{4}\))↔Multiply by 4

Math Language: Translating to Algebra
What to do on this tab Algebra is like a secret code - read the Turn-Around Rule box and the keyword table, then translate each phrase into algebraic form. You get two tries per item.
The "Turn-Around" Rule

Be careful with "less than" and "subtracted from." These are "turn-around" phrases. Example: "5 less than a number" means you start with the number and take 5 away. Correct: \(n - 5\). Incorrect: \(5 - n\).

OperationNatural Language PhrasesSymbol
AdditionSum, increased by, plus, total, added to, more than\(+\)
SubtractionDifference, decreased by, minus, less than, subtracted from\(-\)
MultiplicationProduct, times, of, twice (\(\times2\)), triple (\(\times3\))\(\cdot\) or \(()\)
DivisionQuotient, divided by, per, out of, ratio\(\div\) or fraction bar
EqualsIs, equals, yields, amounts to, is the same as\(=\)

Word Problem Application
What to do on this tab Follow the 4-Step Method for each problem: define the variable, write the equation, solve, and interpret. Enter just the final answer for each problem below - you get two tries.
The 4-Step Method
  1. Define the Variable: choose a letter and state exactly what it represents.
  2. Write the Equation: use translation keywords to build your equation.
  3. Solve: use inverse operations to isolate the variable, performing the same operation on both sides.
  4. Interpret: write your final answer as a complete sentence with units.
Resolved Example: The Taxi Ride

A taxi ride costs a flat fee of \$5.00 plus \$2.00 per mile. If the total cost was \$17.00, how many miles was the ride?

1. Define Variable: Let \(m\) = the number of miles.

2. Equation: \[2m + 5 = 17\]

3. Solve: \[2m = 12 \rightarrow m = 6\]

4. Interpret: The taxi ride was 6 miles long.
