Day 1: Research & Modeling
Budgets Are Value Statements 0-8 min

In the book Counting the Cost, the authors explain that a budget is much more than just a list of numbers. A budget is a value statement. It tells the story of what a person or a group cares about most. When we act as stewards, we are managing resources that don't just belong to us - they are meant to serve the common good.

In this project, you and a partner will become "Financial Stewards" for a local charity. You will research how they help the community, then build a mathematical model to show how they spend their money. By using linear functions, you can predict how much it costs to help 10 people, 100 people, or even 1,000 people. Your goal is to show how math can be a tool for justice and kindness.

Every linear model has two parts: a fixed cost that stays the same no matter how many people are served, and a variable cost that grows with every person helped. Written in function notation, a budget becomes f(x) = mx + b - the slope m is the cost per person, and the y-intercept b is the fixed cost.

Remember: The Linear Model
  • Fixed cost (the y-intercept, b) never changes, no matter how many people are served.
  • Variable cost (the slope, m) is multiplied by the number of people served, x.
  • A charity's total monthly cost is f(x) = mx + b - "the cost to serve x people."
