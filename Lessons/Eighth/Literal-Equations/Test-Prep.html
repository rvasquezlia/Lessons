The Core Idea

A literal equation solves exactly like a numeric one - subtract/add, then multiply/divide, in reverse order. The only difference is that every letter you aren't solving for gets treated like a number: move it with the properties of equality, never combine it with a letter it doesn't match.

If your target variable...Do this
Appears once, multiplied or dividedUndo with one inverse operation.
Has something added or subtracted, tooUndo the addition/subtraction first, then the multiplication/division.
Appears more than onceFactor it out of every term it's in, then divide by what's left.

Critical Thinking

Suppose you tried to solve \(A = P + Prt\) for \(P\) by dividing only the second term by \((rt)\), instead of factoring \(P\) out of the whole right side first. Would that give the same answer? Explain why factoring is required whenever the target variable appears in more than one term.
