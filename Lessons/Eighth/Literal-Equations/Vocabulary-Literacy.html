Key Vocabulary: Literal Equations
A literal equation is an equation with more than one letter - like the perimeter and area formulas you already know.

Working With Literal Equations

Literal Equation

An equation with two or more variables (letters), instead of just one.

Example: \(d = rt\)
Formula

A literal equation that describes a real relationship between quantities - it's a rule, not a one-time equation.

\(A = lw\) is the formula for the area of a rectangle
Solve for a Variable

Rearrange a formula so one specific letter is alone on one side, written in terms of the others.

Solving \(d = rt\) for \(t\) gives \(t = \frac{d}{r}\)
Isolate

Get a variable alone on one side using inverse operations - the exact same move you use in a numeric equation.

\(P = 2l + 2w \rightarrow w = \frac{P - 2l}{2}\)
Subject of the Formula

The variable that ends up alone. Once you've solved for it, that variable is "the subject."

\(t = \frac{d}{r}\) has \(t\) as its subject
Treat It Like a Number

Every letter you're not solving for: move it like a number. Never combine mismatched letters.

Solving \(y=mx+b\) for \(x\): \(m\) and \(b\) act like numbers
Distribute

Multiply a factor by every term inside parentheses - works the same with letters as with numbers.

\(a(b + c) = ab + ac\)
Factor Out

Pull a shared variable out front, turning a sum into a product - the reverse of distributing.

\(P + Prt = P(1 + rt)\)
