Topic Overview: Solving Linear Equations

Savvas enVision Algebra 1, Lessons 1-2 & 1-3. Solving equations with the variable on one side, on both sides, and classifying solutions as one, none, or infinitely many.

  • Six sections, not five daily lessons: Vocabulary & Literacy, Explanation, Practice Set, Word Problems, Test Prep, and this Teacher's Guide.
  • Every section returns to the same core move: whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other.
SectionPurposeSuggested Time
Vocabulary & LiteracyGlossary of every term needed, plus translating words to algebra.~20 min, before instruction begins
ExplanationDirect instruction - every procedure broken into steps, with fully worked examples (carousels, step-reveals, number line, resolved applications).~50-60 min, in one or two sittings
Practice SetComputation-only guided practice, seven tabs from warm-up through strategy challenge and error analysis.~60-90 min, can span several days
Word ProblemsEvery item a real scenario - mixture, work-rate, cost comparisons, savings/billing classification, and challenge word problems.~45-60 min
Test PrepFull review, an error-analysis checkpoint, a submit-only Mixed Practice (digital, serves as the assessment), and a readiness reflection.~45 min, the day before the assessment
Topic Objectives
  • Explain why each step used to solve a linear equation preserves equality.
  • Solve linear equations using properties of equality and inverse operations.
  • Solve equations containing variables on both sides.
  • Recognize equations with one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.
  • Apply linear equations to mathematical and real-world situations.
Standards & Objectives

HSA.CED.A.1, HSA.REI.A.1, HSA.REI.B.3, HSN.Q.A.2, CCSS.Math.Content.8.EE.C.7 & MP.1, MP.2, MP.3, MP.4, MP.7.

Multidisciplinary connections:

  • Science & Mathematical Modeling - equations as models for measurable relationships.
  • Engineering & Logical Reasoning - maintaining equivalence through transformations.
  • Communication & Collaboration - defending a classification or strategy choice.
  • Critical Thinking & Self-Assessment.
Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Forgetting to distribute to every term inside parentheses, not just the first one.
  • Applying an operation to only one side of the equation instead of both.
  • Stopping as soon as the variable cancels, instead of checking whether what remains is true or false.
  • Losing a negative sign when subtracting a variable term from both sides.
  • Confusing "5 less than a number" (\(n-5\)) with "5 minus a number" (\(5-n\)) - the Turn-Around Rule on the Vocabulary & Literacy page addresses this directly.

Lesson-by-Lesson Guide

What each of the six pages covers, and what each tab inside it is for. Answer keys for every graded item already live inline on the student page itself – sign in there as a teacher and it unlocks automatically (see the howto note above), so this tab is about what to teach, not what to grade.

1. Review

Refreshes the "simplify" skills this topic's solving procedure assumes students already have – combining like terms, the distributive property, and signed-number arithmetic – before ever asking them to isolate a variable. Gate progress with Tab 6's graded check before moving on.

TabWhat it's about
1. Why Review This?The hook – separates solving an equation into two phases ("simplify" then "isolate") and makes clear the "isolate" phase is the only genuinely new skill; "simplify" is review, not new content.
2. VocabularyA fast recap of terms used on this page only – the full equation-solving glossary (solution, inverse operations, etc.) lives on the unit's own Vocabulary & Literacy page instead.
3. Like TermsRe-teaches the "variable part must match exactly" rule for combining terms – the single most common reason a simplification goes wrong before a student ever touches the equation-solving steps.
4. Distributive PropertyRe-practices distributing a number across every term inside parentheses, not just the first one – the single biggest predictor of Explanation/Practice Set errors if shaky here.
5. Integer Rules RecapRefreshes signed-number arithmetic (the same table used in Rational Numbers / Operations with Rationals) – every later step in this topic depends on getting a sign right.
6. Are You Ready?The graded checkpoint (2 tries per item) – this is the one tab worth checking before assigning the rest of the unit.
7. ResourcesUngraded self-check list ("You're Ready When You Can...") plus extra practice links for a student who wants more before moving on.
2. Vocabulary & Literacy

Builds the actual vocabulary and translation skill this topic needs. Assign this before Explanation if a class is noticeably stronger at pure computation than at word problems.

TabWhat it's about
1. Key VocabularyGlossary of every term used across this topic (solution, inverse operation, coefficient, etc.) – the reference the other two tabs draw on.
2. Translating Words to AlgebraGraded practice turning a phrase into algebraic form, anchored by the Turn-Around Rule – distinguishing "5 less than a number" (\(n-5\)) from "5 minus a number" (\(5-n\)), the exact misconception named on the Overview tab.
3. Quick Vocabulary CheckA drag-and-drop term/definition/example match-up, not a fill-in-the-blank quiz – students drag or tap-select each term onto its matching definition and example. A term only earns credit once both are matched, and there's no attempt limit, so this is a low-stakes self-check rather than a graded assessment.
3. Explanation

Worked-examples-only, nothing graded here – model the carousels and step-reveals live rather than assigning this page cold. This is the page students should watch you work through, not just read silently.

TabWhat it's about
1. FoundationsProperties of equality and inverse operations – the "whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other" rule this entire topic is built on.
2. Variable on One SideWorked carousel examples: simplify each side first (distribute/combine), then isolate the variable using inverse operations, one step at a time.
3. Variables on Both SidesThe one new move this tab adds: collect every variable term onto one side first – after that, it's Tab 2's procedure again.
4. Classifying SolutionsThe one/none/infinitely-many decision: simplify both sides fully. A false statement (\(6=7\)) means no solution, a true statement (\(4=4\)) means infinitely many, anything else is one solution with a specific value.
5. Worked ApplicationsThe 4-step method (define the variable, write the equation, solve, interpret) worked start-to-finish on real scenarios – the bridge to Word Problems.
4. Practice Set

The main graded computation page – seven tabs moving from one-step warm-up through a student-choice strategy challenge and error analysis. Expect this to span more than one class period.

TabWhat it's about
1. Warm-UpSix one-step/two-step equations, each needing a single inverse operation – a fluency check before the harder items ahead.
2. Variable on One SideGuided practice (work shown on paper/notebook, final answer typed in) – matches Explanation Tab 2's procedure.
3. Variables on Both SidesSame guided-practice format, now requiring the "collect variable terms first" move; students type "no solution" or "infinite" when the variable cancels out.
4. Mixed Equations BankEvery equation type from this topic mixed together with no label telling students which kind they're about to solve – the real test of whether they recognize the situation, not just execute a known procedure.
5. Classify the SolutionStudents simplify and select a classification (one/none/infinitely many), entering \(x\)'s value when there is one.
6. Strategy ChallengeStudent-choice: pick one of four themed groups (A: Variables on Both Sides, B: Distribute First, C & D: Watch the Variable – the no-solution and infinite-solution cases), solve the flagship equation, then 7 more in the same style. See the Metacognition & Discussion tab for how to run this as a group activity.
7. Error AnalysisGiven someone else's solved-but-wrong equation, find the first incorrect step, name the step number, and give the correct solution.
5. Word Problems

Applies the whole topic to real scenarios – mixture/rate problems first (most scaffolded), then cost/savings comparisons that lean on the classify-the-solution skill, then open-ended challenge problems.

TabWhat it's about
1. Mixture & Work-RateChemistry-mixture and combined-work-rate scenarios – a genuine multidisciplinary tie-in (science/engineering modeling), not just a renamed algebra problem.
2. Cost ComparisonsSet two total-cost expressions equal, solve for the break-even point, then use that number to make a real recommendation – which plan is cheaper, and when.
3. Savings & BillingDirectly reuses the classify-the-solution skill: a false-statement comparison means the two plans never meet (no solution); a true-statement comparison means the two plans are identical in disguise (infinitely many).
4. More Word ProblemsStraight 4-Step-Method practice (define/write/solve/interpret) across a fresh batch of scenarios; students enter just the final answer.
5. Challenge Word ProblemsOne level harder – more setup before an equation even appears (consecutive integers, an age problem), rewarding careful reading over quick pattern-matching.
6. Test Prep

The assessment-prep page – treat Tab 3 (Mixed Practice) as a real graded checkpoint, since it deliberately reveals nothing on submit.

Metacognition & Discussion

Reflection prompts and small-group discussion questions to use alongside any of the six pages – none of this is graded content on a student page, it's for you to drop into a warm-up, an exit ticket, or a mid-lesson pause.

Reflection Prompts (use as an exit ticket or journal stem)
  • "What's one step today where I could have lost a negative sign, and how did I make sure I didn't?"
  • "When I got 'no solution' or 'infinitely many,' how did I know which one it was – what in my own work told me?"
  • "If I had to explain the distributive property to a friend who keeps forgetting to multiply the second term, what would I say?"
  • "Which problem today took me more than one try? What did I actually change between my first attempt and my second?"
  • "Where in real life might an equation with a variable on both sides actually show up?"
Leveled Small-Group Discussion Prompts
TierPrompt
Building UnderstandingTake turns explaining to your partner: why does "whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other" keep an equation balanced? Use a real example to show what goes wrong if you skip a side.
ApplyingAs a group, solve \(4(x-2)+3x=5(x+1)-3\) two different ways (e.g. one person distributes first, another combines differently if possible). Do you land on the same answer? Where could either path have gone wrong?
ExtendingWrite one equation with variables on both sides that simplifies to a false statement (no solution), and a second one that simplifies to a true statement (infinitely many). Trade with another group – can they tell which is which just by looking at your setup, before they solve it?
Using Practice Set's Strategy Challenge (Tab 6) as a Group Activity

Each strategy group (A: Variables on Both Sides / B: Distribute First / C & D: Watch the Variable) targets a different piece of the skill progression – consider assigning groups deliberately rather than letting every student pick the same one, so you get a genuine spread when you compare answers as a class afterward. A student's progress on whichever group they choose is saved automatically and reviewable in the teacher dashboard's Full Submission Log exactly like any other graded item; the on-screen picker itself doesn't yet remember a prior choice across a reload (a known limitation, not a data-loss issue – nothing a student submits is ever lost, they'd just see the picker screen again).

Explanation-Tier Challenges

C1. Solve \(3(2x - 5) - 4 = 2(x + 7)\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(6x - 15 - 4 = 2x + 14\). Combine: \(6x - 19 = 2x + 14\). Subtract \(2x\): \(4x - 19 = 14\). Add 19: \(4x = 33\). Divide by 4: \(x = \mathbf{\frac{33}{4} = 8.25}\).

C2. Solve \(-(x - 6) = 2x + 3\).

Show solution

Distribute the negative: \(-x + 6 = 2x + 3\). Add \(x\): \(6 = 3x + 3\). Subtract 3: \(3 = 3x\). Divide by 3: \(x = \mathbf{1}\).

C3. Classify \(4(x + 3) = 2(2x + 6)\). Is this always, sometimes, or never true?

Show solution

Distribute both sides: \(4x + 12 = 4x + 12\). The two sides are identical, so this is always true - infinitely many solutions.

C4. Solve \(5(2x + 1) - 7 = 3x - 30\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(10x + 5 - 7 = 3x - 30\). Combine: \(10x - 2 = 3x - 30\). Subtract \(3x\): \(7x - 2 = -30\). Add 2: \(7x = -28\). Divide by 7: \(x = \mathbf{-4}\).

C5. Solve \(-2(x + 4) = 3x + 7\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(-2x - 8 = 3x + 7\). Add \(2x\): \(-8 = 5x + 7\). Subtract 7: \(-15 = 5x\). Divide by 5: \(x = \mathbf{-3}\).

Practice-Set-Tier Challenges

C1. Solve \(2(3x - 1) + 5 = 3(x + 4) - x\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(6x - 2 + 5 = 3x + 12 - x\). Combine: \(6x + 3 = 2x + 12\). Subtract \(2x\): \(4x + 3 = 12\). Subtract 3: \(4x = 9\). Divide by 4: \(x = \mathbf{\frac{9}{4} = 2.25}\).

C2. Solve \(5x - 2(x - 3) = 2x + 20\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(5x - 2x + 6 = 2x + 20\). Combine: \(3x + 6 = 2x + 20\). Subtract \(2x\): \(x + 6 = 20\). Subtract 6: \(x = \mathbf{14}\).

C3. Classify \(6x + 11 = 6x - 4\). Explain what the result tells you.

Show solution

Subtract \(6x\) from both sides: \(11 = -4\), a false statement. This equation has no solution - no value of \(x\) can make it true.

C4. Classify \(4(x - 3) + 2x = 6(x - 2)\).

Show solution

Distribute and combine the left side: \(4x - 12 + 2x = 6(x-2) \rightarrow 6x - 12 = 6x - 12\). Identical sides - infinitely many solutions.

C5. Solve \(7x - 2(x + 1) = 3x + 8\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(7x - 2x - 2 = 3x + 8\). Combine: \(5x - 2 = 3x + 8\). Subtract \(3x\): \(2x - 2 = 8\). Add 2: \(2x = 10\). Divide by 2: \(x = \mathbf{5}\).

C6. Classify \(3(x + 2) - x = 2x + 6\).

Show solution

Distribute and combine the left side: \(3x + 6 - x = 2x + 6 \rightarrow 2x + 6 = 2x + 6\). Identical sides - infinitely many solutions.

C7. Classify \(4x + 9 = 4x - 2\).

Show solution

Subtract \(4x\): \(9 = -2\), false. No solution.

C8. Solve \(7 - 2(x - 3) = x + 1\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(7 - 2x + 6 = x + 1\). Combine: \(13 - 2x = x + 1\). Add \(2x\): \(13 = 3x + 1\). Subtract 1: \(12 = 3x\). Divide by 3: \(x = \mathbf{4}\).

C9. Classify \(6x - 1 = 2(3x + 4)\).

Show solution

Distribute the right side: \(6x - 1 = 6x + 8\). Subtract \(6x\): \(-1 = 8\), a false statement. No solution.

C10. Classify \(9 - 3(x - 1) = 15 - 3x\).

Show solution

Distribute the left side: \(9 - 3x + 3 = 15 - 3x \rightarrow 12 - 3x = 15 - 3x\). Add \(3x\): \(12 = 15\), a false statement. No solution.

Word-Problems-Tier Challenges

C1. Two consecutive integers have a sum of 47. Write and solve an equation to find both integers.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the smaller integer: \(x + (x + 1) = 47 \rightarrow 2x + 1 = 47 \rightarrow 2x = 46 \rightarrow x = \mathbf{23}\). The integers are \(\mathbf{23}\) and \(\mathbf{24}\).

C2. Solve \(2(4x - 3) = 3(2x + 1) + 1\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(8x - 6 = 6x + 3 + 1 \rightarrow 8x - 6 = 6x + 4\). Subtract \(6x\): \(2x - 6 = 4\). Add 6: \(2x = 10\). Divide by 2: \(x = \mathbf{5}\).

C3. Three consecutive even integers have a sum of 72. Write and solve an equation to find all three.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the smallest: \(x + (x+2) + (x+4) = 72 \rightarrow 3x + 6 = 72 \rightarrow 3x = 66 \rightarrow x = \mathbf{22}\). The integers are \(\mathbf{22}\), \(\mathbf{24}\), and \(\mathbf{26}\).

C4. Two numbers differ by 12 and have a sum of 58. Write and solve an equation to find both numbers.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the smaller number: \(x + (x + 12) = 58 \rightarrow 2x + 12 = 58 \rightarrow 2x = 46 \rightarrow x = \mathbf{23}\). The numbers are \(\mathbf{23}\) and \(\mathbf{35}\).

C5. Solve \(4(x - 2) + 3 = 2x + 9\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(4x - 8 + 3 = 2x + 9\). Combine: \(4x - 5 = 2x + 9\). Subtract \(2x\): \(2x - 5 = 9\). Add 5: \(2x = 14\). Divide by 2: \(x = \mathbf{7}\).

Test-Prep Enrichment for Early Finishers

These are original problems, unrelated to the graded assessment, safe to hand out to students who finish early.

C1. Classify \(5(x + 2) - 3x = 2x + 10\).

Show solution

Distribute and combine the left side: \(5x + 10 - 3x = 2x + 10 \rightarrow 2x + 10 = 2x + 10\). Identical sides - infinitely many solutions.

C2. Solve \(9 - 4(x - 2) = 2x - 7\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(9 - 4x + 8 = 2x - 7\). Combine: \(17 - 4x = 2x - 7\). Add \(4x\): \(17 = 6x - 7\). Add 7: \(24 = 6x\). Divide by 6: \(x = \mathbf{4}\).

C3. Solve \(6(x - 1) - 2x = 3x + 2\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(6x - 6 - 2x = 3x + 2\). Combine: \(4x - 6 = 3x + 2\). Subtract \(3x\): \(x - 6 = 2\). Add 6: \(x = \mathbf{8}\).

C4. A parent is 4 times as old as her son; in 6 years she will be 3 times as old as her son will be then. Write and solve an equation for the son's current age, \(x\).

Show solution

\(4x + 6 = 3(x + 6)\). Distribute: \(4x + 6 = 3x + 18\). Subtract \(3x\): \(x + 6 = 18\). Subtract 6: \(x = \mathbf{12}\). The son is 12 now.

C5. Classify \(5x - 3(x - 2) = 2x + 6\).

Show solution

Distribute the left side: \(5x - 3x + 6 = 2x + 6 \rightarrow 2x + 6 = 2x + 6\). Identical sides - infinitely many solutions.
