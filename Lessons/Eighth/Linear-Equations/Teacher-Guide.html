Topic Overview: Solving Linear Equations
Savvas enVision Algebra 1, Lessons 1-2 & 1-3. This topic covers solving equations with the variable on one side, equations with the variable on both sides, and classifying solutions as one, none, or infinitely many. It is organized into six sections instead of five daily lessons: Vocabulary & Literacy, Explanation, Practice Set, Word Problems, Test Prep, and this Teacher's Guide. Every section returns to the same core move: whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other.
SectionPurposeSuggested Time
Vocabulary & LiteracyGlossary of every term needed, plus translating words to algebra.~20 min, before instruction begins
ExplanationDirect instruction - every procedure broken into steps, with fully worked examples (carousels, step-reveals, number line, resolved applications).~50-60 min, in one or two sittings
Practice SetComputation-only guided practice, seven tabs from warm-up through strategy challenge and error analysis.~60-90 min, can span several days
Word ProblemsEvery item a real scenario - mixture, work-rate, cost comparisons, savings/billing classification, and challenge word problems.~45-60 min
Test PrepFull review, an error-analysis checkpoint, a submit-only Mixed Practice (printable-worksheet style), and a readiness reflection.~45 min, the day before the assessment
Topic Objectives
  • Explain why each step used to solve a linear equation preserves equality.
  • Solve linear equations using properties of equality and inverse operations.
  • Solve equations containing variables on both sides.
  • Recognize equations with one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.
  • Apply linear equations to mathematical and real-world situations.
Standards & Objectives

HSA.CED.A.1, HSA.REI.A.1, HSA.REI.B.3, HSN.Q.A.2, CCSS.Math.Content.8.EE.C.7 & MP.1, MP.2, MP.3, MP.4, MP.7.

Multidisciplinary connections: Science & Mathematical Modeling (equations as models for measurable relationships); Engineering & Logical Reasoning (maintaining equivalence through transformations); Communication & Collaboration (defending a classification or strategy choice); Critical Thinking & Self-Assessment.

Common Misconceptions to Watch For
  • Forgetting to distribute to every term inside parentheses, not just the first one.
  • Applying an operation to only one side of the equation instead of both.
  • Stopping as soon as the variable cancels, instead of checking whether what remains is true or false.
  • Losing a negative sign when subtracting a variable term from both sides.
  • Confusing "5 less than a number" (\(n-5\)) with "5 minus a number" (\(5-n\)) - the Turn-Around Rule on the Vocabulary & Literacy page addresses this directly.

Explanation-Tier Challenges

C1. Solve \(3(2x - 5) - 4 = 2(x + 7)\).

Show solution

Distribute: \(6x - 15 - 4 = 2x + 14\). Combine: \(6x - 19 = 2x + 14\). Subtract \(2x\): \(4x - 19 = 14\). Add 19: \(4x = 33\). Divide by 4: \(x = \mathbf{\frac{33}{4} = 8.25}\).

C2. Solve \(-(x - 6) = 2x + 3\).

Show solution

Distribute the negative: \(-x + 6 = 2x + 3\). Add \(x\): \(6 = 3x + 3\). Subtract 3: \(3 = 3x\). Divide by 3: \(x = \mathbf{1}\).

C3. Classify \(4(x + 3) = 2(2x + 6)\). Is this always, sometimes, or never true?

Show solution

Distribute both sides: \(4x + 12 = 4x + 12\). The two sides are identical, so this is always true - infinitely many solutions.

C4. Solve \(5(2x + 1) - 7 = 3x - 30\).

Show solution

C1. Two consecutive integers have a sum of 47. Write and solve an equation to find both integers.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the smaller integer: \(x + (x + 1) = 47 \rightarrow 2x + 1 = 47 \rightarrow 2x = 46 \rightarrow x = \mathbf{23}\). The integers are \(\mathbf{23}\) and \(\mathbf{24}\).

C3. Three consecutive even integers have a sum of 72. Write and solve an equation to find all three.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the smallest: \(x + (x+2) + (x+4) = 72 \rightarrow 3x + 6 = 72 \rightarrow 3x = 66 \rightarrow x = \mathbf{22}\). The integers are \(\mathbf{22}\), \(\mathbf{24}\), and \(\mathbf{26}\).

Show solution
