Key Vocabulary: Solving Linear Equations
Every term below shows up somewhere in this topic - in the Explanation, in the Practice Set, or in a Word Problem. Read through them once before you start, and come back any time a word trips you up.

Parts of an Equation

Expression

A combination of numbers, variables, and operations with no equal sign.

Example: \(4x - 3\)
Equation

A mathematical sentence stating that two expressions are equal, joined by an "=" sign.

Example: \(3x + 5 = 20\)
Linear Equation

An equation where the variable is only raised to the first power (no \(x^2\), no \(x\) under a root) - its graph is a straight line.

Example: \(2x - 7 = 9\)
Variable

A letter that represents an unknown or changing number.

Example: the \(x\) in \(2x - 7 = 9\)
Term

A single number, variable, or product/quotient of numbers and variables, separated from other terms by \(+\) or \(-\).

In \(4x + 7\), the terms are \(4x\) and \(7\)
Coefficient

The number multiplied by a variable in a term.

In \(6x\), the coefficient is \(6\)
Constant

A term that is just a fixed number, with no variable attached.

In \(3x + 9\), \(9\) is the constant
Like Terms

Terms with the exact same variable part, so they can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

\(5x\) and \(-2x\) are like terms; \(5x\) and \(5x^2\) are not

Solving Moves

Properties of Equality

Rules that let you add, subtract, multiply, or divide both sides of an equation by the same amount without changing its solution.

If \(a = b\), then \(a + c = b + c\)
Inverse Operations

Opposite operations that "undo" each other - used to peel operations away from a variable.

Addition ↔ Subtraction; Multiplication ↔ Division
Distributive Property

Multiplying a factor by every term inside a set of parentheses.

\(a(b + c) = ab + ac\)
Isolate the Variable

The goal of solving: use inverse operations until the variable stands alone on one side of the equation.

\(3x = 15 \rightarrow x = 5\)
Solution

A value that makes an equation true when substituted for the variable.

\(x = 6\) is the solution of \(x + 9 = 15\)
Solution Set

The complete collection of every value that solves an equation - just one value, no values, or every real number.

"All real numbers" or "∅" (empty set)

Classifying Solutions

One Solution

The equation simplifies until exactly one value of the variable makes it true.

\(2x + 5 = 3x - 1 \rightarrow x = 6\)
No Solution

The variable cancels out completely, leaving a false statement. No value of the variable works.

\(2(x+3) = 2x + 7 \rightarrow 6 = 7\)
Infinitely Many Solutions

The variable cancels out completely, leaving a true statement. Every real number works.

\(4(x+1) = 4x + 4 \rightarrow 4 = 4\)
Identity

Another name for an equation with infinitely many solutions - both sides are always equal, no matter the value of the variable.

\(2(x+3) = 2x + 6\)

The "Turn-Around" Rule

Be careful with "less than" and "subtracted from." These are "turn-around" phrases. Example: "5 less than a number" means you start with the number and take 5 away. Correct: \(n - 5\). Incorrect: \(5 - n\).
